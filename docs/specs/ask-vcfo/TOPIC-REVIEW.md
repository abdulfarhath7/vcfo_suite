### After incorporation (`after-incorporation`)

- **Question:** What happens after incorporation?
- **Used by:** suggestion client-after-incorporation, step post-3, step post-4, step post-5, step post-6, step post-2, glossary INC-20A, glossary ADT-1
- **Applies to:** company; domestic and foreign
- **Audience:** both · **Category:** incorporation · **Version:** 1

**Normal:** Once the Certificate of Incorporation is issued, the company opens a bank account, receives its share capital, issues share certificates and files a few early forms, such as INC-20A to declare commencement of business and ADT-1 for its first auditor.

**Simple:** After the company exists, we open its bank account, bring in the money for shares and file a few starting forms.

**More detail:** The first months after incorporation follow a set order. The board holds its first meeting and appoints the first auditor within 30 days. The company opens a bank account and the subscribers pay for their shares. Share certificates (SH-1) are issued within 60 days. INC-20A, the declaration of commencement of business, is filed within 180 days and confirms the share money was received. Where a foreign parent paid for the shares, FC-GPR is reported to the RBI within 30 days of the shares being issued.

**Why it matters:** Several of these have fixed legal deadlines counted from the incorporation date, and the bank account and share money you arrange drive most of them.

**Visual (steps):** Step 1: Open the company bank account. Step 2: Receive share capital. Step 3: Issue share certificates, form SH-1. Step 4: Declare commencement of business, form INC-20A. Step 5: Appoint the first auditor, form ADT-1. Step 6: Report foreign investment (foreign parent), form FC-GPR.

### FC-GPR (`fc-gpr`)

- **Question:** What is FC-GPR?
- **Used by:** suggestion client-fc-gpr, suggestion admin-fc-gpr, suggestion super-fc-gpr, step post-7, glossary FEMA, glossary FC-GPR
- **Applies to:** all legal forms; foreign
- **Audience:** both · **Category:** foreign-investment · **Version:** 1

**Normal:** FC-GPR is the report an Indian company files with the Reserve Bank of India after it issues shares to a foreign investor. It is filed on the RBI's FIRMS portal within 30 days of issuing the shares.

**Simple:** FC-GPR tells the Reserve Bank of India that a foreign company bought shares in your Indian company.

**More detail:** Under FEMA, foreign investment into an Indian company must be reported to the RBI. When the company issues shares to a person resident outside India, it files Form FC-GPR through the FIRMS portal within 30 days of the allotment. The filing includes the foreign inward remittance details, the bank's KYC report on the investor, a valuation certificate and a declaration from the company secretary. Late filing attracts a late submission fee.

**Why it matters:** Your parent company's share money is foreign investment, so this report keeps the investment on record with the RBI and avoids penalties.

**Visual (keyFacts):** Filed with: Reserve Bank of India, FIRMS portal. When: Within 30 days of issuing shares. Needs: Remittance details, KYC report, valuation certificate. Applies to: Shares issued to a foreign investor.

### GST basics (`gst-basics`)

- **Question:** What is GST, and do we need it?
- **Used by:** suggestion client-gst, step reg-4, glossary GST, glossary GSTIN
- **Applies to:** all legal forms; domestic and foreign
- **Audience:** both · **Category:** tax · **Version:** 1

**Normal:** GST (Goods and Services Tax) is India's tax on the supply of goods and services. A business registers when its turnover crosses the threshold or when the law makes registration compulsory, for example for inter-state supplies of goods or exports under LUT.

**Simple:** GST is India's sales tax. Some businesses must register for it; your project lead will confirm if yours does.

**More detail:** GST replaced most indirect taxes in 2017. Registration gives the business a GSTIN, lets it charge GST on sales and claim credit for GST paid on purchases. Registration is compulsory once aggregate turnover crosses the threshold (generally ₹40 lakh for goods and ₹20 lakh for services, lower in some states) and in specific cases regardless of turnover, such as inter-state supply of goods or supplying through e-commerce operators. Exporters usually also file a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) to export without paying tax upfront. Registered businesses file regular returns such as GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B.

**Why it matters:** Whether your company must register depends on what it sells, where and how much. That is a decision your firm confirms for you.

**Visual (keyFacts):** What it is: Tax on supply of goods and services. Registration: Above turnover threshold, or compulsory in listed cases. Identifier: GSTIN (15 characters). Regular returns: GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B.

**Citations:**
- CGST Act 2017, sections 22–24 (registration) (`cgst-act-2017-s22-24`)
- GST portal — https://www.gst.gov.in (`gst-portal`)

### SPICe+ Part A (`spice-plus-part-a`)

- **Used by:** suggestion client-spice-part-a, step pre-1, step pre-4, step pre-5, glossary SPICe+
- **Applies to:** company; domestic and foreign
- **Audience:** both · **Category:** incorporation · **Version:** 1
- **Effort label:** About 15 minutes

**Normal:** SPICe+ is the single online form the Ministry of Corporate Affairs uses to incorporate a company in India. Part A reserves the company's name: we propose up to two names and the Registrar checks they are unique and allowed.

**Simple:** Part A is where we ask the government to approve your company's name.

**More detail:** SPICe+ (form INC-32) has two parts. Part A is name reservation: the proposed names, the main business activity and the type of company are submitted, and the Registrar of Companies either approves a name or sends it back for changes. An approved name is held for 20 days, during which Part B (the incorporation itself) must be filed. Part A can also be filed together with Part B in one go. The details you give in Client Details feed the name application your project lead files.

**Why it matters:** Nothing else in incorporation can be filed until a name is approved, so the details you give now set the pace for the whole setup.

**Visual (flow):** Stage 1 of 3, Part A (Name approval), you are here. Stage 2 of 3, Part B (Incorporation), next. Stage 3 of 3, Incorporated (Certificate issued), next.

### Capital structure (`capital-structure`)

- **Question:** What is authorised and paid-up capital?
- **Used by:** step pre-13
- **Applies to:** company; domestic and foreign
- **Audience:** client · **Category:** your-project · **Version:** 1
- **Effort label:** About 5 minutes

**Normal:** Authorised capital is the most share capital the company may issue under its MOA. Paid-up capital is the part the subscribers actually pay for their shares at incorporation.

**Simple:** Authorised capital is the upper limit; paid-up capital is the money actually put in for shares.

**More detail:** The capital clause of the Memorandum of Association states the authorised capital and how it divides into shares of a face value, for example 10,000 shares of ₹10 each. The subscribers take some or all of those shares at incorporation and pay for them after the bank account opens; that amount is the paid-up capital. Authorised capital can be increased later, but government fees at incorporation depend on it.

**Why it matters:** These numbers go into the MOA, the subscription sheet and the share certificates, and they decide how much money has to reach the company's bank account.

- **Applies to:** company; domestic and foreign
- **Audience:** client · **Category:** your-project · **Version:** 1
- **Effort label:** About 15 minutes per director

**Normal:** Every proposed director is identified to the Ministry of Corporate Affairs with identity and address proof and a photograph. New directors are given a Director Identification Number (DIN) through the incorporation form.

**Simple:** Each director shares ID and address proof so the government can identify them.

**More detail:** For each proposed director we collect identity proof (PAN for Indian nationals, passport for foreign nationals), address proof and a photograph. ### Registered office (`registered-office`)

- **Question:** What is a registered office?
- **Used by:** step pre-14
- **Applies to:** all legal forms; domestic and foreign
- **Audience:** client · **Category:** your-project · **Version:** 1
- **Effort label:** About 10 minutes

**Normal:** The registered office is the company's official address in India, where government notices are sent. It needs proof of address, such as a recent utility bill, and the owner's permission if the premises are not owned.

**Simple:** It is the company's official address in India for government letters.

**More detail:** Every company must have a registered office from the date of incorporation. The address is given in SPICe+ Part B with a utility bill no older than two months and, where the premises are rented or borrowed, a rent agreement or a no-objection letter from the owner. The address also decides which Registrar of Companies and which state rules apply. Moving it later needs a form such as INC-22 and sometimes more approvals.

**Why it matters:** The address sets the state your company is registered in and the documents you gather now are attached to the incorporation filing.
