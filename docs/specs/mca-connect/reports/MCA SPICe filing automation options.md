# Automate SPICe+ preparation, keep people at gates

MCA publishes no filing API, no offline utility, no bulk upload and no approved-vendor scheme for SPICe+ on V3. So no third party can lawfully file SPICe+ Part A or Part B for SBC. The research found no exception. Every lawful route ends in one human act: a Business User logs in with their own ID, a person types the captcha and OTP, each signer signs with their own DSC, and a person pays the fee. Third-party tools can help only before and after that act. Before it, they can prepare and check data, generate documents and fill fields in the person's own session. After it, they can read status emails. Under SBC's rules, only a few options are **Allowed** today. These are the firm's own data and document pipeline, local PDF tools, MCA's own signer, the data.gov.in company dataset, status-email ingestion and deadline reminders. Every tool that touches the live V3 portal is **Needs CS opinion**, and that includes the firm's own Chrome extension, VCFO Assist. Two things make it so: MCA's full terms of use could not be read, and rule 2 bars live-portal use before written CS approval. Captcha solvers, OTP forwarding, unattended bots, stored client logins, shared or cloud-held DSCs and agent platforms with built-in solvers are **Prohibited**. The market has nothing better than VCFO Assist. The only vendor product that pushes SPICe+ into V3, SAG Infotech's Gen CompLaw, does not say how it does so. So SBC's main job is to get a written CS opinion on attended filling in the user's own session, and to fit hard guards into the extension.

## How to read this report, and where the evidence is thin

The report applies the firm's three rules to every option. Rule 1 comes first: nothing may be illegal, a written CS or counsel opinion decides, and an unclear position counts as prohibited. Rule 2: no tool may touch the live MCA portal before written CS approval. Rule 3 bans five acts outright: captcha or OTP bypass, signing for a signer, use of another person's login or DSC without valid authority, false or test data on the portal, and calls to private portal endpoints. Each option gets one of three verdicts.

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Allowed** | Lawful on the evidence found. It needs no live-portal contact, or only the contact a professional already makes when filing by hand. |
| **Needs CS opinion** | The position is unclear, or the option touches the live portal. Treat it as prohibited until a written CS or counsel opinion clears it. |
| **Prohibited** | It breaks rule 3 or a cited law, or a vendor's own material shows a banned feature. |

The research has real limits, and the reader must weigh every verdict against them. Several primary hosts were blocked in the research sessions. These were mca.gov.in, cca.gov.in, icsi.edu, negd.gov.in, directory.apisetu.gov.in, ai.icai.org, learn.quicko.com, and the vendor sites saginfotech.com, complyrelax.com, resources.probe42.in, meon.co.in and chromewebstore.google.com. So many facts rest on search-engine extracts and on secondary sources such as practitioner blogs, law-firm notes and competitor pages, not on a full read of the primary page. The most important unread document is the full MCA V3 Terms and Conditions and Website Policy. That text decides whether even attended automation counts as access "with permission". Several named vendors returned no results at all: Leagully, CSTrax, AutoROC, Winman CS/ROC, Spectrum ROC, EasyOffice ROC, Taxmann ROC, TaxCloud India, ClearTax MCA filing, Corpzo, Registrationwala, Capitall, Tally-integrated ROC tools and iCompliance. They are **unconfirmed, not ruled out**. Some other tools were named but not researched before the budget ran out. These were Blue Prism, Axiom.ai, Bardeen, Zapier, Make, browser-use, Browserbase, Multion, Magical, Text Blaze, Fillr, Kissflow, Pantasign, Sify SafeScrypt, SignDesk, Signzy eSign, Docusign India, Karza/Perfios, Tofler, Zauba, Setu, Docassemble, Docupilot, PandaDoc, Spellbook, Adobe PDF Services and Apryse. This report gives them no verdict.

Some sources conflict, and the report does not resolve these conflicts. The V2 shutdown date appears as 18 June 2025, 14 July 2025 and 30 June 2026. The attachment cap appears as 6,400 KB and as 6 MB. Sources disagree on whether Class 2 DSCs still work. They disagree on whether SPICe+ allows Pay Later. Two versions of the CCA Interoperability Guidelines appear (v4.0 and v4.1). The fine under IT Act s.72A appears both as ₹5 lakh and as ₹25 lakh. The research notes also differ among themselves. The tooling note rates attended RPA and the firm's extension "Allowed". The legal note rates browser automation of V3 as "needs opinion", because the MCA terms were unreadable. This report follows rule 1 and uses the stricter verdict.

## Eight legal anchors decide every verdict

No MCA rule found expressly allows or bans third-party filing software, bots or scraping. No court case on automated MCA filing was found either. The verdicts therefore rest on general rules that already bind the people in the filing. The anchors below come up again and again in the option tables.

## MCA's official channels end at the browser form

On V3, the only sanctioned filing path is a web form. A Business User with a linked DSC fills it in a logged-in session ([InstaFinancials](https://blog.instafinancials.com/2025/06/19/mca-launches-final-38-company-forms-from-july-14-2025/)). Once Part B is submitted, the linked forms open: eMoA (INC-33), eAoA (INC-34), INC-9, URC-1 and AGILE-PRO-S. The user downloads them, signs them with DSC and uploads them ([MCA FAQ, SPICe+ linked filings](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/mca/global/en/help-faq/faqs/company-services/incorporation/spiceplus-linked-filings.html)). The linked registrations for PAN, TAN, EPFO, ESIC, GSTIN, the bank account and Professional Tax are government-to-government links inside SPICe+. Outside software cannot call them ([taxguru](https://taxguru.in/?p=774344)). One blog claims V3 has "bulk upload, status polling and data retrieval APIs". It has no official backing, and nothing should be built on it ([IncorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/mca-v3-portal-filing-changes-2026)).

## Automation tools pass only when a person holds every gate

Attended automation runs in a session the person has opened, and it pauses for the person at login, captcha, OTP and submit. It does nothing that a careful person typing quickly could not do. That is why VCFO Assist's design is the strongest pattern found anywhere in the market. But two gaps remain. No MCA text found allows it, and the full V3 terms were unreadable. Rule 2 also bars live use before written CS approval. So every tool in this group that touches the live portal gets **Needs CS opinion**, not Allowed. No source shows any RPA vendor running a production bot on MCA forms. The documented Indian cases are GST, TRACES and Income Tax ([Skcript](https://consulting.skcript.com/automation/use-cases/rpa-for-gst-filing-automation); [dqindia](https://www.dqindia.com/improving-efficiency-and-accuracy-in-tax-function-using-robotics-process-automation-rpa)). So buying an RPA platform means SBC builds and maintains the MCA flow itself on someone else's platform.

## Signing, payment and document tools: only the signer's own token is safe

SPICe+ is authenticated by digital signature under Rule 8 of the Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules ([ca2013.com](https://ca2013.com/rule-8-companies-registration-offices-and-fees-rules-2014/)). Each subscriber and director registers as a Business User, and no one may share a DSC ([Razorpay Rize](https://razorpay.com/rize/blogs/mca-spice-plus-faqs)). V3 signs through MCA's local signer bridge, emBridge, which practitioners call "the single most common failure point" ([calcguru](https://calcguru.in/?p=9795)). Any change to a PDF after a signature invalidates that signature ([UPDF](https://updf.com/fr/pdf-form/pdf-signature-invalid/)). So a third-party signing platform carries a technical risk as well as a legal one. Nothing found shows that MCA accepts a SPICe+ PDF signed outside its own flow. The firm cannot test this on the live portal, because rule 3 bans test data there. The answer must come from MCA guidance, the vendor, or a real filing after CS approval.

| Signing option | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| MCA emBridge. Each signer uses their own token and types their own PIN | **Allowed** | This is the required baseline. It matches CCA custody rules. |
| The firm sends the exact MCA-generated PDF, and each remote signer signs it on their own token without editing or re-saving it | **Allowed** | Each act of signing is personal. The risk is technical, so the instructions to signers must forbid edits, flattening and "Save As". |
| Leegality DSC token utility (local port, PC only, bulk sign up to 500) or eMudhra emSigner token workflows, used for the firm's own attachments such as board resolutions, consents and NOCs ([Leegality](https://support.leegality.com/portal/en/kb/articles/dsc-token-signing-journey); [eMudhra](https://emudhra.com/en/blog/digitally-sign-pdf-file-using-electronic-signature-software-emsigner)) | **Allowed** | Each signer uses their own token. Confirm with MCA or the vendor that MCA accepts the output before relying on it. |
| The same platforms, or KDK Zen PDF, Capricorn Signer Pro or Adobe bulk signing, used on the SPICe+ form PDF itself ([KDK](https://www.kdksoftware.com/zen-pdf/); [Adobe India](https://www.adobe.com/in/acrobat/roc/blog/bulk-digital-signatures.html)) | **Needs CS opinion** | MCA acceptance is unproven, and the platform may rewrite the file. |
| A person's own cloud or HSM DSC (eMudhra remote signing) ([emSign](https://emsign.com/resources/collaterals/Remote-Signing-Made-Simpler.pdf)) | **Needs CS opinion** | It is unconfirmed whether MCA's signer accepts it. Its custody model differs from the token model. |
| Aadhaar OTP eSign for SPICe+ | **Needs CS opinion** | Only vendor marketing claims that MCA accepts it ([eSign.AI](https://www.esign.ai/blog/india-mca-esignature-for-new-company-registration)). Treat it as not accepted. |
| Leegality "Doc Signer" organisational PFX, or any one certificate that signs for several people ([Leegality](https://www.leegality.com/feature-explainers/doc-signer)) | **Prohibited** | Each subscriber and director must sign with their own DSC. |
| The firm keeps a client's token in its office with written consent, and the client types the PIN | **Needs CS opinion** | This is common practice. But it conflicts with the CCA "custody of the subscriber" text. |
| Firm staff hold clients' tokens and PINs and sign for them, or a DSC server or USB hub in a data centre | **Prohibited** | This is signing for a signer. It carries s.66C and s.448 exposure and breaks the CCA custody rule. |
| A vault that the client cannot take its DSC or credentials back out of | **Prohibited** | ICAI treats a lien over portal credentials as misconduct. |

Payment stays a manual step for each SRN. Practitioner sources say the signed PDF must be uploaded within **15 days of SRN generation**, and the fee must be paid within **7 days of upload**. Otherwise the SRN lapses ([Razorpay Rize](https://razorpay.com/rize/company-registration/document-required/spice-part-b)). Part B must follow within 20 days of name approval ([Razorpay Rize FAQs](https://razorpay.com/rize/blogs/mca-spice-plus-faqs)). Offline Pay Later stopped on 8 June 2025 ([taxguru](https://taxguru.in/?p=966094)). A June 2026 guide lists net banking, NEFT, UPI and cards on V3 ([SPOTONZ](https://www.spotonz.com/blog/mca-v3-portal-guide-india/)). No API or bulk-payment route for MCA fees was found.

| Payment option | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A person pays online on V3 for each SRN | **Allowed** | This is the official route. |
| VCFO Suite records the SRN and sends email (plus an optional WhatsApp nudge) before the 15-day and 7-day limits | **Allowed** | It fits the existing compliance calendar. Email stays the system of record. |
| A person batches NEFT payments on the bank side | **Allowed** if V3 offers NEFT | NEFT is a V2-era mode ([MCA V2 NEFT](https://www.mca.gov.in/MinistryV2/neft.html)). Its V3 status and challan validity are unconfirmed. |
| The firm pays a client's SRN from the firm's own account | **Needs CS opinion** | No source covers it. |
| A bot or AI agent pays with bank credentials and bank OTPs | **Prohibited** | It needs OTP bypass and handling of financial credentials. |

Document tools add little, because SBC already generates docx in `src/lib/incorporation-docs/` and `src/lib/board-resolution/`. On V3, e-MoA and e-AoA are web forms, so a generator matters mainly for attachments ([taxguru](https://taxguru.in/?p=718919)). The real need is a pre-signing PDF step. It converts files to PDF, downsamples scans, and keeps each file under the cap, which one source gives as **6,400 KB** ([Quicko snippet](https://learn.quicko.com/mca-dsc-settings)) and another as 6 MB.

| Document option | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| The firm's existing docx pipeline | **Allowed** | Already built. Reuse comes first. |
| pdf-lib (local, in an Inngest job). It converts and compresses files before anyone signs and never touches a signed file | **Allowed** | Data stays on the firm's own systems. |
| Hosted PDF APIs such as Adobe PDF Services or Apryse | **Needs CS opinion** | Client KYC data would go to a foreign cloud. Cross-border processing under DPDP s.16 is unclear. These tools were not researched. |
| Docassemble, Docupilot, PandaDoc, Spellbook | No verdict | Not researched. They duplicate built work. |

## Commercial data APIs are lawful only if their source is

Many vendors sell MCA lookups for prefill and verification. None found offers a name-availability (RUN) endpoint or an SRN-status endpoint ([LegalWiz](https://www.legalwiz.in/blog/how-to-check-company-name-availability-on-mca-portal)). The few published prices are about ₹8 per MCA lookup and ₹2 per PAN check at Truid ([Truid](https://truid.pages.dev/)), and US$0.05 to 0.20 per record for Apify OGD actors ([Apify](https://apify.com/publicrows/mca-company-master)). The key fact is hidden. Most vendors do not say whether they use licensed data or fetch live from captcha-gated V3 pages. If a vendor scrapes past the captcha, the firm would buy the product of a captcha bypass. Director data is also personal data. In an RTI reply dated 11 July 2025, MCA said it does not share directors' contact details with private entities ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/rti-reveals-mca-discloses-who-has-access-to-company-directors-personal-data-1435440)).

| Data option | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Apify actors that state they read the OGD dataset ([Apify OGD](https://apify.com/nexgendata/india-mca-companies-director-filings-ogd)) | **Allowed** | Licensed open data. The firm can also call data.gov.in directly with a free key. |
| Surepass (CIN, DIN, signatories, name-to-CIN, struck-off) ([Surepass](https://surepass.io/mca-data-apis-cin-din/)), Decentro ([Decentro](https://decentro.tech/api-hub/mca-verification-api)), Qorpiq ([Qorpiq](https://developers.qorpiq.com/docs/mca-company-master-data)), Gridlines, IDfy, AuthBridge, Truid, Infyner, meon, companydata.com, Probe42 | **Needs CS opinion** | Their data source is undisclosed. Each needs a written statement of source and licence, plus a processor contract. |
| Apify actors that are not based on OGD, and the araystech GitHub wrapper around compdata.in (last updated December 2019) ([GitHub](https://github.com/araystech/mca-data-api)) | **Needs CS opinion** (not recommended) | Their source is unknown or stale. |
| Any vendor that sells director phone numbers or emails | **Prohibited** | MCA does not share this data. Its presence points to unlawful harvesting ([BestMediaInfo](https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/mediainfo-digital/heres-how-the-ministry-of-corporate-affairs-open-database-is-fueling-a-spam-bazaar-9585974)). |
| Any vendor or AI agent that gets director Aadhaar, PAN or address proofs with no written processor contract | **Prohibited** | It breaks DPDP s.8(2). |
| Sending full Aadhaar numbers or images to any vendor | **Needs CS opinion** | The Aadhaar Act limits were not verified. Use masked Aadhaar where MCA accepts it. |

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. SBC started by asking which vendor could automate SPICe+. The answer is that no lawful product can file it, and the strongest design in the market is the one SBC already has. That design is an extension that fills fields in the professional's own session and stores no credentials. Other tools are either offline preparers or tools that hide how they reach the portal, and the hidden mechanism is exactly what the firm's rules care about. So the limit is not technology. It is one unread document, MCA's full terms, and one missing signature, the CS's written approval.

That gives the firm a clear order of work. First, build the Allowed off-portal pieces, which save most of the double typing with no legal exposure. Second, put the guards into VCFO Assist as code. Third, take the eleven CS questions to the CS as one bundle. The vendor questions are cheap to send and could change two verdicts. A written answer from SAG Infotech could move Gen CompLaw to Allowed or to Prohibited. A data vendor that confirms it uses OGD data could join the shortlist. Each unconfirmed vendor remains an open question until it answers in writing.
