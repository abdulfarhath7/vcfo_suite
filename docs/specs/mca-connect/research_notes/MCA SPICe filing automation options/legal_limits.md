# Legal and regulatory limits on automating MCA V3 (SPICe+) filings with third-party software (India, as of November 2026)

Scope note. This is desk research, not legal advice. Every place below marked "needs CS/counsel opinion" or "unclear" should be treated as **prohibited** under the firm's rule (unclear = prohibited) until an opinion is obtained.

Research limit. In this session the hosts mca.gov.in, icsi.edu and cca.gov.in could not be fetched directly (the session's network blocked them). Their content is cited from search-engine extracts of those pages, not from a full read. A CS should open each primary link and confirm the quoted text before relying on it. The MCA V3 "Terms and Conditions" / "Website Policies" page text in particular could not be read in full.

Tags used in the Inferences sections: **[ALLOWED]**, **[OPINION]** (needs a CS or counsel opinion; treat as prohibited until then), **[PROHIBITED]**.

## 1. MCA V3 portal terms, user agreement and MCA circulars/notices on third-party software, fake portals and user-ID misuse

### Takeaway
No MCA rule found in this research expressly allows or expressly bans third-party filing software, bots or scraping on the V3 portal. The portal's binding text that could be found does three things. It ties each user ID to one person and one DSC. It tells users not to share OTPs or passwords. It warns that only mca.gov.in is official. So any tool that logs in as a client, shares a login, or acts as a "portal" in front of MCA is outside what MCA permits, and the rest is unclear.

### Cited Findings
- MCA V3 registration page: the site's terms say certain sections or pages "may carry separate terms", and those additional terms govern in a conflict. Governing law is Indian law, and the courts of New Delhi have exclusive jurisdiction over disputes about use of the site. — [MCA, Register on MCA V3 Portal](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/mca/global/en/foportal/fo-user-registration.html)
- The same page defines a "Business User" category (companies, LLPs, directors, designated partners, and professionals such as insolvency professionals and liquidators). Business users get the basic registered-user services plus extra services by role. ICAI/ICSI members register with membership type, institute and membership number, which ties the professional ID to the person. — [MCA, Register on MCA V3 Portal](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/mca/global/en/foportal/fo-user-registration.html)
- MCA V3 FAQs (go-live 5 March 2022 version): a DSC is not required to log in to V3, but for signing "only 1 DSC can be associated with 1 User ID". — [MCA, V3 FAQs 20-02-2022](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/dam/mca/pdf/v3FAQs_20022022.pdf); updated FAQs dated 26-04-2022 mirrored at [fintaxblog](https://fintaxblog.com/wp-content/uploads/files/mca-update-dt-26-04-2022-consolidated-updated-faqs-usage-login-at-mca-portal-v3-new-llp-application.pdf) (not read in full)
- MCA-21 V3 Login & Registration FAQ ("Do's and Don'ts", hosted by ICSI) tells users: "Do not share OTP/password with any unauthorized user." — [ICSI, FAQs MCA-21 V3 Login & Registration and Do's & Don'ts](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/FAQs_MCA-21_Login_Registration.pdf)
- MCA advisory (reported mid-2026): stakeholders should verify all communications only through https://www.mca.gov.in before acting; MCA "never contacts stakeholders via mobile calls or WhatsApp regarding non-compliance or enforcement actions"; official email only from @mca.gov.in addresses; report frauds at cybercrime.gov.in. — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mca-warns-citizens-against-phishing-scams-fake-officials-and-call-forwarding-fraud); [TaxGuru, Beware of Fraudulent Communications Impersonating MCA Officials](https://taxguru.in/company-law/beware-fraudulent-communications-impersonating-mca-officials.html); [MCA21India on X, Email scam alert](https://x.com/MCA21India/status/1948674272236351772); [CAclubindia](https://www.caclubindia.com/news/mca-warns-stakeholders-against-fake-calls-whatsapp-messages-and-phishing-scams-26667.asp)
- Commentary on the same advisory: some fake websites claim to offer "MCA filing, compliance verification, penalty removal, company status update or document correction", and phishing pages ask for login ID, password, OTP, PAN, Aadhaar and bank details. — [Sweksha](https://sweksha.com/mca-fraud-alert-beware-of-fake-calls-whatsapp-messages-emails-and-phishing-links/) (secondary)
- Third-party vendors' own terms put responsibility for password confidentiality and "all activities occurring under the account" on the user (example: MCA Suite, a private tool, not MCA). — [MCA Suite Terms of Service](https://www.mcasuite.com/term-of-use/)

### Inferences
- **[PROHIBITED]** A vendor or tool that holds a client's (or director's) MCA user ID and password and logs in as them. It breaks the "do not share OTP/password" instruction and the one-person/one-ID model, and it is also the IT Act s.66C risk (section 4 below).
- **[PROHIBITED]** Any tool that presents itself as an MCA portal or a "filing gateway" to clients, or that asks clients to type MCA credentials or OTPs into a non-mca.gov.in page. MCA's own advisory names this pattern as phishing.
- **[ALLOWED]** Software that runs entirely on the firm's side (drafting, validation, document generation, checklists, prefilling a local copy of SPICe+ data) where a human professional logs in with **their own** ID and submits on mca.gov.in. Nothing found restricts this.
- **[OPINION]** Browser automation (RPA, Playwright-style bots) that drives the V3 portal under the firm's own professional ID with a human present. No MCA text found forbids it, but the full Terms and Conditions page was not readable here, and "separate terms" for some sections may apply. Treat as prohibited until the full terms are read and a CS signs off.
- The absence of an MCA API or authorised-intermediary scheme for company filings (none was found) means there is no "approved vendor" route to rely on.

### Gaps
- Full text of the MCA V3 "Terms and Conditions", "Website Policies", "Hyperlinking Policy" and any business-user agreement: could not be fetched (mca.gov.in blocked in this session). Whether they contain an express bar on automated access, crawling or bots is **unknown**. Treat as unknown, not as permission.
- No MCA general circular (2019–2026) specifically on third-party filing software or misuse of user IDs was found. The search surfaced only the anti-phishing advisories. A CS should search MCA's "Notices and Circulars" and "Latest News" pages directly.
- Whether MCA's V3 portal has a captcha on login and on form upload, and whether MCA has stated any position on captcha solvers, was not confirmed.

## 2. Companies Act 2013 and Companies (Incorporation) Rules 2014: who may file and certify SPICe+, declarations, and penalties for false filing

### Takeaway
SPICe+ is a signed statutory document. The professional certification and the subscriber and director declarations are personal acts by named people, made with their own DSC. False or suppressed particulars attract fraud liability under s.447 through s.7(5), s.7(6) and s.448, and that liability reaches the certifying professional. Automation can prepare the form but cannot replace these personal acts.

### Cited Findings
- Rule 38: the application for incorporation "shall be in FORM No. INC 32 (SPICe) alongwith e-Memorandum of Association (e-MOA) in Form No INC-33 and e-Articles of Association (e-AOA) in Form no. INC-34". — [ICAI Navi Mumbai mirror of Rule 38](https://navimumbai.icai.org/detail/30381.aspx)
- The 2020 amendment substituted "Simplified Proforma for Incorporating Company Electronically Plus (SPICe+)" in rule 38 sub-rules (1), (2), (3), (4), (7) and (9), effective 23 February 2020, and replaced rule 9 so that name reservation goes through the RUN web service or SPICe+. — [AMS Shardul, Incorporation Rules amended to provide for SPICe+](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/companies-incorporation-rule-2014-amended-to-provide-for-form-spice); [ca2013.com, amendment notification PDF](https://ca2013.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/rule_22022020.pdf)
- Rule 14 / Form INC-8: the s.7(1)(b) declaration is made by an advocate, a Chartered Accountant, Cost Accountant or Company Secretary in practice who is engaged in the formation of the company. — [ca2013.com, Rule 14 (valid as on 05/08/2026)](https://ca2013.com/rule-14-companies-incorporation-rules-2014/); [LawSikho](https://lawsikho.com/blog/documents-required-for-company-registration/) (secondary)
- s.7(5): a person who furnishes false or incorrect particulars, or suppresses material information he is aware of, in any document filed with the Registrar in relation to registration of a company, is liable for action under s.447. — [TaxGuru, Section 7](https://taxguru.in/?p=66795); [CourtBook, Companies Act s.7](https://courtbook.in/companies-act-2013/section-7)
- s.7(6): where it is proved after incorporation that the company was got incorporated by false or incorrect information or suppression of material facts, the promoters, the first directors and the persons making the s.7(1)(b) declaration are each liable under s.447. s.7(7) lets the NCLT, on application, pass orders including striking off or winding up. — [TaxGuru, Section 7](https://taxguru.in/?p=66795); [LegalMantra, Section 7](https://legalmantra.net/blog-detail/Section-7-of-The-Companies-Act-2013)
- s.448: a person who, in any return, report, certificate, financial statement, prospectus, statement or other document required by the Act, makes a statement false in any material particular knowing it to be false, or omits a material fact knowing it to be material, is liable under s.447. — [LegalMantra, Section 448](https://legalmantra.net/blog-detail/Section-448-of-The-Companies-Act-2013)
- Commentary: a professional who certifies a form for a person disqualified under s.164(2) is exposed under ss.447 and 448. — [TaxGuru, Disqualification of director](https://taxguru.in/company-law/disqualification-director-struck-companies.html)
- MCA disqualification practice: after the 2017 disqualifications, the DIN and DSC of disqualified directors could not be used to file any document. — [TaxGuru, HC directs ROC to remove disqualification](https://taxguru.in/company-law/hc-directs-roc-remove-disqualification-director-active-companies-reactivate-dins-dscs.html)

### Inferences
- **[PROHIBITED]** Any tool or vendor that affixes the certifying professional's DSC, or a subscriber's or director's DSC, without that person personally reviewing and signing. The certificate and declarations are statements by that person under s.7(1)(b)/(c) and s.448.
- **[PROHIBITED]** Any "auto-fill and auto-submit" that submits SPICe+ data the certifying CS/CA/CMA has not checked against source documents. ICAI and ICSI treat certification without verification as misconduct (section 5), and false particulars carry s.447 exposure.
- **[ALLOWED]** Software that prepares SPICe+, e-MOA, e-AOA, AGILE-PRO-S and INC-9 content, runs validations, and hands a complete draft to the professional who then reviews, signs and files.
- **[OPINION]** Where the vendor (not the firm) is the one that pre-scrutinises or "certifies readiness". The certifying professional stays fully liable, so the firm needs a CS view on what review evidence it must keep.

### Gaps
- Exact current text of s.447 penalties: search results did not confirm. From general knowledge (unverified here), s.447 provides imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years and a fine from the amount involved up to three times that amount, with a 3-year minimum where public interest is involved. A CS must confirm on indiacode.nic.in.
- Rule 8 (name availability criteria) and Rule 15/INC-9 wording were not verified from a primary source. The search result that placed the subscriber declaration under rule 15 could not be checked against rule text.
- Whether the 2025–2026 amendments to the Incorporation Rules or the V3 SPICe+ form changed who may certify (for example adding or removing advocates or CMAs) was not checked.

## 3. Information Technology Act 2000: ss.43, 66, 66C, 66D, 72A; CERT-In directions; legal status of DSC; CCA rules on DSC custody

### Takeaway
The CCA framework says the Class 3 DSC token is in the subscriber's custody and "exclusive control", the private key cannot be copied or backed up, and the CA never holds it. Using another person's DSC, password or unique identification feature "fraudulently or dishonestly" is a s.66C offence. Access without the owner's permission is civil liability under s.43 and a crime under s.66 if done dishonestly. A firm or vendor holding clients' DSC tokens is therefore at odds with CCA rules even with consent, and a cloud agent that signs with a client's DSC is the highest-risk pattern.

### Cited Findings
- CCA FAQ: Class 2 and Class 3 keys must be stored in a FIPS 140-2 Level 2 validated token "which is in the custody of the subscriber". — [CCA, FAQ](https://cca.gov.in/faq.html)
- CCA Certificate Policy for India PKI: the subscriber's private key is generated by the end subscriber, so the CA does not need to deliver a private key; subscriber private keys held in hardware may not be backed up or copied; subscriber private signature keys shall not be archived by the CA. — [CCA, X.509 Certificate Policy for India PKI](https://cca.gov.in/sites/files/pdf/guidelines/CCA-CP.pdf)
- CCA subscriber agreement: the subscriber affirms that the token holding the private key is "under her/his exclusive control and physically detached from computer when it is not in use". — [Licensed CA Subscriber Agreement v1.0 (eKYC), ncode repository](http://ncode.in/repository/CCA-SUBAGRMT-eKYC.pdf)
- CCA Electronic Signature Application Integration Guidelines place safe custody of the token on the subscriber. — [CCA, ESAIG v1.0](https://cca.gov.in/sites/files/pdf/guidelines/CCA-ESAIG.pdf)
- CA practice statements: the CA "is never in possession of the Subscriber's private signing keys". — [CSC e-Governance CPS 4.1.0 (on cca.gov.in)](https://cca.gov.in/sites/files/pdf/Licensed-CA/CSC_CPS_4.1.0.pdf)
- eSign / eKYC route is a different model: under it the CA generates the key pair on the account holder's authorisation, and the token-storage requirements are stated as not applicable. — [SafeScrypt CPS 4.1.1](https://www.safescrypt.com/drupal/pdf/SIFY_CA_CPS4.0.1.pdf); [ncode subscriber agreement](http://ncode.in/repository/CCA-SUBAGRMT-eKYC.pdf)
- CCA ordered that DSCs be generated only on hardware tokens with effect from 25 November 2013. — [Corporate Law Reporter](https://corporatelawreporter.com/cca-ordered-mandating-generation-dscs-tokens-w-e-f-25th-nov-2013/)
- CCA token guidelines: users must change the default PIN before a certificate is downloaded to a token, and PIN reset is not available. — [certificate.digital](https://www.certificate.digital/articles/133/everything-about-cca-guidelines-on-usb-tokens-in-india/) (secondary)
- CCA Interoperability Guidelines for DSC: one search extract shows version 4.0 dated 10 February 2026; another shows version 4.1 dated 16 June 2026. — [CCA, Guidelines page](https://cca.gov.in/guidelines.html) (versions conflict between extracts; check the live page)
- s.66C (identity theft): fraudulent or dishonest use of another person's electronic signature, password or other unique identification feature; up to 3 years' imprisonment and fine up to ₹1 lakh. — [ApniLaw, s.66C and DSC misuse](https://www.apnilaw.com/legal-articles/acts/section-66c-it-act-identity-theft-digital-signature-misuse-explained/) (commentary says DSC misuse to sign documents falls within s.66C); [journalism.university](https://journalism.university/contemporary-scenario-of-digital-media/regulating-cyberspace-india-it-act-2000) (penalty)
- Allahabad High Court (order dated around 6 September 2026, Krishna Kant Pandey v. State of UP): creating an email ID in another's name is prima facie not identity theft under s.66C because the section covers only use of the named credentials (electronic signature, password, unique identification feature). This is an interim view on a writ petition. — [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/allahabad-high-court/krishna-kant-pandey-and-another-v-state-of-up-through-principal-secretary-home-lucknow-and-others-1621487); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/allahabad-high-court/allahabad-hc-email-id-another-name-prima-facie-identity-theft-66c-it-act-548769); [order PDF](https://www.livelaw.in/pdf_upload/2026/09/06/krishna-kant-pandey-and-another-vs-state-of-up-thru-prin-secy-home-lko-and-others-698395.pdf)
- Bombay High Court has required specific allegations of fraudulent or dishonest use before s.66C applies. — [lawtext.in headnote](https://lawtext.in/judgement.php?bid=50512)
- s.43: civil liability (compensation) for access to a computer resource without the owner's permission, regardless of intent; no imprisonment under s.43 alone. s.66: the same acts done dishonestly or fraudulently are a crime, up to 3 years and/or fine up to ₹5 lakh. — [ApniLaw, s.43 explained](https://www.apnilaw.com/legal-articles/acts/section-43-it-act-explained-hacking-and-unauthorized-access-to-computer-systems/); [journalism.university](https://journalism.university/?p=253749)
- s.66D (cheating by personation using a computer resource): up to 3 years and fine up to ₹1 lakh. — [journalism.university](https://journalism.university/?p=253749)
- s.72A (disclosure of personal information obtained under a lawful contract without consent): originally up to 3 years or fine up to ₹5 lakh — [Meghalaya Police circular 36/2011](https://megpolice.gov.in/sites/default/files/Circular36_2011.pdf); a later source says the Jan Vishwas Act 2023 changed the fine to up to ₹25 lakh — [NASSCOM community](https://new.nasscom.in/communities/public-policy/update-amendments-jan-vishwas-amendment-provisions-act-2023-information?page=3) (conflict; verify on indiacode)
- CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022 (effective 27 June 2022): report listed cyber incidents within 6 hours of noticing them; keep ICT system logs for 180 days (in India per the directions; an FAQ reading allowing offshore logs if produced on demand is reported but unconfirmed); applies broadly to service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporates and government organisations. — [PSA Legal](https://psalegal.com/new-cert-in-directions-overview-and-implications/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/it-and-internet/1202576/everything-you-need-to-know-about-indias-new-guidelines-related-to-cyber-incident-reporting-by-cert-in); [Sumo Logic](https://www.sumologic.com/?p=5198) (log-location conflict)

### Inferences
- **[PROHIBITED]** A vendor or cloud service that physically holds clients' DSC tokens, or a "DSC server/USB hub" in a data centre that signs on behalf of directors, subscribers or the certifying professional. CCA texts require subscriber custody and exclusive control, and copying keys is barred. Consent from the director does not cure the CCA custody requirement; it only reduces s.66C intent risk.
- **[PROHIBITED]** Any arrangement in which the firm or vendor signs with a DSC whose subscriber did not personally approve that specific document (s.66C/66D exposure, plus s.448 under the Companies Act).
- **[OPINION]** The firm keeping a client's DSC token in its office for convenience, with written consent and the client entering the PIN or approving each signing. This is common practice, but it conflicts with the CCA "custody of the subscriber" text and ICAI/ICSI place responsibility for misuse on the DSC owner and the professional (section 5). Needs a CS view; until then treat as prohibited for the firm's own process design.
- **[OPINION]** Using an eSign (Aadhaar OTP-based) signature in place of a token. It is a separate CCA model, but MCA must accept it for the specific form; acceptance for SPICe+ was not confirmed here.
- **[ALLOWED]** A local signing helper on the signer's own machine, with the token plugged in by the signer and the PIN entered by the signer (this is how MCA's own emSigner works).
- CERT-In duties apply to the firm and to any vendor that hosts the data: the contract with a vendor should require 6-hour incident reporting to the firm and CERT-In, and 180-day logs.

### Gaps
- IT Act s.3 (authentication by digital signature) and s.5 (legal recognition of electronic signatures): primary text was not fetched. Their standard effect is that a document is authenticated by the subscriber's own digital signature, which supports the custody reading, but this should be confirmed on indiacode.nic.in.
- No CCA guideline found that speaks directly to DSCs "held by intermediaries" or filing agents. The custody rule is inferred from the FAQ, CP and subscriber agreement. The Interoperability Guidelines PDF (v4.0/4.1, 2026) could not be read.
- No Indian judgment found squarely holding that a professional's use of a client's DSC with informal consent is or is not a s.66C offence.

## 4. ICSI and ICAI professional conduct: holding clients' DSCs and MCA logins, and outsourcing filing

### Takeaway
Neither institute's guidance note squarely on holding client DSCs was found. Disciplinary orders show three firm rules: the professional is responsible for any unauthorised use of their own DSC; certifying e-forms without verifying documents is misconduct; and a professional may not hold a client's DSC or portal credentials as a lien. Outsourcing the filing does not move any of this liability to the vendor.

### Cited Findings
- ICSI Chartered Secretary (July 2024) ethics case study: "it is the duty and obligation of a Company Secretary in Practice to prevent any unauthorized use of his or her Digital Signatures/DSC"; the Disciplinary Committee held that only the respondent was responsible for use of his own DSC, and criticised him for reporting misuse to police only after regulators asked. — [ICSI, CSJ July 2024](https://icsi.edu/media/webmodules/CSJ/July-2024/35.pdf)
- ICSI appellate matter: a practising CS was held guilty of professional misconduct for certifying filings without verifying the required documents (due-diligence failure). — [Praveen Kumar Kanungo v. Disciplinary Committee ICSI](https://taxguru.in/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/Praveen-Kumar-Kanungo-Vs.-Disciplinary-Committee-ICSI.pdf); see also [ABCAUS, diligence extends to purpose of law](https://abcaus.in/icsi/diligence-required-by-practising-company-secretary-extends-to-purpose-of-law.html/amp)
- ICSI (August 2026) case study: practising CSs are expected to collate data from the client's records and previous filings. — [ICSI, CSJ Aug 2026](https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/CSJ/Aug2026/29.pdf)
- ICSI advisory to members to refrain from unethical practices. — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/?p=7919)
- ICAI Disciplinary Committee: a chartered accountant "cannot exercise a lien over a client's documents, accounting records or statutory portal credentials for non-payment of professional fees"; the CA, whose firm withheld a client's DSC token and portal logins, was fined ₹50,000. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/chartered-accountant/icai-fines-ca-rs-50-000-tds-defaults-withholding-client-records.html)
- ICAI reprimand (CA Puneet Jain, INC-22): "the responsibility for safeguarding a digital signature rests with its owner as per the Information Technology Act"; the form lacked proof of ownership required by the Incorporation Rules. — [TaxGuru, CA reprimanded for misuse of DSC in MCA filing](https://taxguru.in/chartered-accountant/ca-reprimanded-misuse-dsc-mca-filing.html)
- ICAI: a CA whose DSC was used without consent was held not guilty, the Committee noting that he had complained to the National Cyber Crime portal about the misuse. — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/icai-disciplinary-committee-clears-ca-from-professional-misconduct-for-alleged-connivance-in-incorporation-of-shell-companies-read-order/345145)
- ICAI: certifying MCA e-forms without due diligence is misconduct under Item (7), Part I, Second Schedule, Chartered Accountants Act; fines of ₹30,000 and ₹2 lakh reported in separate orders. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/chartered-accountant/icai-reprimands-ca-failing-exercise-due-diligence-professional-duties.html); [StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/icai-levies-rs-2-lakh-penalty-on-ca-for-lapse-in-due-diligence-on-mca-filing-held-guilty-for-professional-misconduct-404467.html); [ICAI Disciplinary Directorate order](https://disc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/138.-DC-219-220-221-12.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[PROHIBITED]** Giving a vendor the certifying professional's DSC or MCA professional login. ICSI places sole responsibility for its use on the CS, and loss of control is itself a disciplinary risk.
- **[PROHIBITED]** Any tool workflow that lets the firm withhold or lock a client's DSC or MCA credentials (for example a vault the client cannot get back out of). ICAI treats lien over portal credentials as misconduct.
- **[OPINION]** Outsourcing preparation of SPICe+ to a vendor (a back office or another firm) with the firm's CS still certifying. Permissible in principle (nothing found against it), but the CS must keep evidence of independent verification, and confidentiality duties under the CS/CA Acts' Schedules apply; needs a CS view on engagement-letter terms.
- **[ALLOWED]** Internal tools that log who prepared, who reviewed and who signed each form, with a client confirmation step. These directly answer the due-diligence findings.

### Gaps
- No ICSI or ICAI guidance note specific to custody of clients' DSCs, sharing MCA logins, or outsourcing e-filing was found. The Code of Conduct clauses (Company Secretaries Act 1980, First and Second Schedules; Chartered Accountants Act 1949, Schedules) were not read in primary form.
- ICMAI (cost accountants) guidance was not searched.

## 5. DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025: sending director/subscriber data (Aadhaar, PAN, address) to a third-party vendor or cloud agent

### Takeaway
The firm is the data fiduciary for client personnel data it collects for incorporation. It may use a vendor or cloud agent only under a valid contract (s.8(2)), stays liable for what the vendor does, and must ensure reasonable security safeguards (s.8(5)), with a maximum penalty of ₹250 crore for a safeguards failure. The detailed Rule 6 security list (including a processor-contract clause) only becomes enforceable about 18 months after notification, around May 2027, but the Act's duties and the IT Act regime apply in the meantime.

### Cited Findings
- DPDP Rules 2025 notified in November 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)). Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 (including the Data Protection Board) in force on notification; Rule 4 (Consent Managers) one year after; Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23 in force 18 months after publication. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/breaking-govt-notifies-final-dpdp-rules-sets-staggered-rollout-with-key-obligations-for-data-fiduciaries-84201.htm); [Mondaq, phased rollout](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1706878/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones); [AMS Shardul alert](https://www.amsshardul.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Regulatory-Alert-Enforcement-of-DPDP-Act-and-Notification-of-DPDP-Rules.pdf)
- Date conflict: sources give the first tranche as 13 or 14 November 2025; one tracker dates Rule 6's commencement to 13 May 2027. — [DPDP Wiki, Rule 6](https://dpdp.myndsolution.com/wiki/rules/rule-6-reasonable-security-safeguards/); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/breaking-govt-notifies-final-dpdp-rules-sets-staggered-rollout-with-key-obligations-for-data-fiduciaries-84201.htm)
- Rule 6 minimum safeguards include encryption, masking, obfuscation or tokenisation; access controls; logs; backups for continuity; and "appropriate contractual provision" with any data processor obliging it to keep reasonable security safeguards. — [DPDP Wiki, Rule 6](https://dpdp.myndsolution.com/wiki/rules/rule-6-reasonable-security-safeguards/); [Mondaq, closer look at DPDP Rules](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1706890/a-closer-look-at-the-dpdp-rules-2025); [BDO India](https://www.bdo.in/getmedia/87552494-85d2-4edb-95da-0f118fdb3052/The-DPDP-Rules_BDO-India_2025.pdf)
- s.8(2): a fiduciary may engage a processor only under a valid contract; the Act does not prescribe clauses, the Rules add the security-safeguard clause. The fiduciary stays answerable for its processor's processing. — [Tsaaro, processor contracts under DPDP](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/processor-contracts-under-dpdp-drafting-a-data-processing-agreement-that-survives-regulatory-scrutiny); [LawSikho, DPA template (April 2026)](https://lawsikho.com/blog/data-processing-agreement-india-dpdp-act-template-clauses-compliance-guide-2026/)
- Schedule penalties: up to ₹250 crore for failure to take reasonable security safeguards (s.8(5)); up to ₹200 crore for failure to notify a breach (s.8(6)). One source attaches ₹250 crore to a missing processor contract, which the Schedule does not support. — [DPDPA, The Schedule](https://dpdpa.com/theschedule.html); [Perfios](https://perfios.ai/?p=67875); [AMLegals](https://amlegals.com/penalties-under-the-digital-personal-data-protection-act2023/)
- Government position (2024, Rajya Sabha): scraping of public user data is covered by the IT Act, IT Rules and DPDP Act. — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/scraping-public-data-in-india-innovation-enabler-or-privacy-threat-) (attribution from search extract; verify)

### Inferences
- **[ALLOWED]** A vendor or cloud host that processes director/subscriber data only on the firm's instructions under a written data processing agreement (purpose limit, security safeguards per Rule 6, breach notice to the firm without delay, deletion on completion, sub-processor approval, audit rights, India log retention per CERT-In).
- **[PROHIBITED]** Sending Aadhaar, PAN, passport or address proofs to a vendor or AI/cloud agent with no written processor contract, or to a vendor that uses the data for its own purposes (that makes it a fiduciary in its own right, outside the client's notice).
- **[OPINION]** Sending full Aadhaar numbers or Aadhaar images to any vendor at all. The Aadhaar Act 2016 (s.29 and the Aadhaar regulations on storage and masking) adds limits beyond DPDP, which were not verified here. Treat as prohibited; prefer masked Aadhaar where MCA accepts it.
- **[OPINION]** Cross-border processing (vendor or model hosted outside India). DPDP s.16 lets the government restrict transfers by notification; whether any notification restricts it now, and whether sectoral rules apply, needs counsel.
- The notice to clients (DPDP s.5) should name the purpose (incorporation filing with MCA) and the use of processors. The notice rule (Rule 3) is in the 18-month tranche, but the Act's lawful-processing duties are already in force as notified.

### Gaps
- Aadhaar Act 2016 s.29 and UIDAI rules on storing or sharing Aadhaar numbers: the search returned nothing usable. Needs a primary read.
- Whether ss.5–8 of the DPDP Act themselves are already in force or wait for the 18-month date: sources tie the substantive duties to the Rules' phasing, but the exact commencement notification for the Act's sections was not read. Counsel should confirm.
- Whether the firm or its clients could be a Significant Data Fiduciary (unlikely for a professional firm) was not examined.

## 6. Court cases and MCA/ROC enforcement involving filing agents, DSC misuse or automated filing

### Takeaway
The largest enforcement wave was the 2021–2023 action against Chinese-linked shell companies: ROC FIRs naming CAs and CSs, an SFIO arrest of the alleged mastermind, and MCA referring about 400 CAs and CSs to their institutes. No case found involved automated filing software as such. The cases turn on professionals certifying false particulars and using dummy directors' DSCs.

### Cited Findings
- MCA recommended disciplinary action against about 400 CAs and CSs for alleged roles in incorporating Chinese-linked shell companies, on inputs including financial intelligence; ICAI said guilt and numbers were yet to be ascertained (2022). — [A2Z Taxcorp, reporting The Hindu](https://a2ztaxcorp.com/400-chartered-accountants-company-secretaries-under-scanner-for-flouting-norms-to-incorporate-chinese-shell-companies/); [BNC24 (19 June 2022)](https://www.bnc24.com/en/world/2022/06/19/news/620/); [CA Sansaar](https://www.casansaar.com/news-MCA/MCA-explores-Disciplinary-Action-against-CA-CS-in-500-Chinese-Companies/13491.html); [Rajput Jain & Associates](https://carajput.com/learn/icai-issue-notice-to-ca-firms-on-violate-company-law-via-chinese-co.html)

- SFIO arrested the alleged mastermind of shell companies with Chinese links; evidence from ROC Delhi's enquiry showed "dummy directors being paid to act in several shell companies" (Tribune, 11 September, year not given in extract; reported as 2022). — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/sfio-arrests-kingpin-of-chinese-shell-firms-430778); [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/sfio-arrests-mastermind-behind-incorporating-shell-companies-with-chinese-links-430652)

### Inferences
- The enforcement pattern punishes the **certifying professional** for what the filing says, whatever tool prepared it. Any vendor tool must keep an audit trail that shows the professional's own verification (site visit evidence for registered office, KYC of subscribers, who signed when).
- **[PROHIBITED]** Using "nominee" or dummy directors' DSCs operated by the firm or vendor. This is exactly the conduct in the shell-company prosecutions.

### Gaps
- No court judgment or ROC order found on automated or bot-driven MCA filing, or on a filing-software vendor.
- Final outcomes (convictions, suspensions) of the 400 referrals and the ROC Mumbai FIRs were not found.
- Indian Kanoon was not searched directly for "digital signature certificate" with "66C" in MCA filings.

## 7. Captcha bypass and scraping: Indian legal position (contract, IT Act s.43(b)) and case law

### Takeaway
India has no scraping statute and no reported judgment on captcha bypass. Commentators agree that the risk rises sharply once a tool gets past a login, defeats an access control such as a captcha, or loads a site heavily; that is where s.43 (civil) and s.66 (criminal if dishonest) bite. Accepted website terms are also a contract. For a government portal, unclear means prohibited.

### Cited Findings
- No Indian statute specifically addresses web scraping, and the law is unsettled; s.43 creates civil liability for access without permission, becoming a s.66 offence when done dishonestly or fraudulently. — [Ikigai Law, legality of data scraping in India](https://www.ikigailaw.com/article/263/legality-of-data-scraping-in-india); [ApniLaw, s.43](https://www.apnilaw.com/legal-articles/acts/section-43-it-act-explained-hacking-and-unauthorized-access-to-computer-systems/)
- Section 43 arguments gain force when a scraper gets past logins, defeats access controls or degrades a site; scraping public pages at modest volume is rarely the problem. — [LawRato](https://lawrato.com/indian-kanoon/civil-law/is-web-scraping-legal-in-india-what-a-business-can-and-cannot-collect-3192); [Advocate Gandhi](https://advocategandhi.com/data-scraping-in-india-legal-perspectives-risks-and-regulatory-framework-explained/)
- Accepted terms of use form a contract, so scraping in breach of them is a contractual claim; some argue it is also unauthorised access under s.43, which is contested. — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/web-scraping-and-personal-data-legal-and-ethical-boundaries-in-ai/); [Privasapien](https://privasapien.com/blog/web-scraping-for-ai-training-data-legal-constraints-under-indias-dpdp-act-and-it-act); [Legal Service India](https://www.legalserviceindia.com/Legal-Articles/scraping-the-surface-the-legal-grey-zone-of-web-scraping-in-ai-development/)
- The leading "public data" authority (hiQ v. LinkedIn) is a US case and not binding in India. — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/scraping-public-data-in-india-innovation-enabler-or-privacy-threat-)
- The MCA V3 site's terms are governed by Indian law with New Delhi courts having exclusive jurisdiction. — [MCA, Register on MCA V3 Portal](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/mca/global/en/foportal/fo-user-registration.html)

### Inferences
- **[PROHIBITED]** Captcha-solving services (human farms or ML solvers) or any technique that defeats MCA's captcha, OTP or session controls. A captcha is an access control, so defeating it is the strongest s.43(a)/(b) fact pattern, and for a government system it also raises "protected system" questions under s.70 if MCA21 has been notified (not verified).
- **[PROHIBITED]** High-volume or unattended crawling of MCA V3 (company master data, filings, ROC documents), even of pages that are public, until the full MCA terms are read. Copying data from MCA in bulk may also be s.43(b) "downloads, copies or extracts".
- **[OPINION]** Low-volume, human-triggered retrieval of the firm's own clients' public master data (for example one company lookup when a lead clicks "refresh"), with no captcha bypass. Probably defensible but unclear; prefer MCA's paid "View Public Documents" and official data products.
- **[ALLOWED]** Using officially published MCA datasets (for example data.gov.in releases) and the firm's own records.

### Gaps
- No Indian case law on captcha bypass or bot access to government portals was found.
- Whether MCA21 is notified as a "protected system" under IT Act s.70 was not checked; if it is, unauthorised access attracts up to 10 years' imprisonment (statutory text not verified here).
- MCA's paid data and API products (if any exist in 2026) and their licence terms were not researched; another researcher may cover official integration routes.
