# Third-party tooling to automate the human-assisted parts of SPICe+ filing on MCA V3 (RPA, AI browser agents, DSC signing, payment, document generation, captcha/OTP)

Research date: 2026-11-05. Desk research only; nothing was installed, signed up for or touched on the live MCA portal.

Method note for the report writer: three primary hosts could not be fetched from this session because the network policy blocked them (`www.mca.gov.in`, `learn.quicko.com`, `ai.icai.org`). Search snippets from those hosts were used where they appeared, but the full official MCA V3 user manuals on DSC and emBridge could not be read. Most MCA procedural facts below therefore rest on practitioner and consultant blogs. They should be checked against the MCA V3 help pages before anything is built on them. The research hit the tool-call budget before covering every named vendor; the uncovered ones are listed in each Gaps section rather than described from memory.

## 1. Form filling and RPA: which tools apply, which run attended in the user's own session, which store credentials, and which are used on Indian government portals

### Takeaway
Attended desktop RPA (Power Automate Desktop, UiPath attended, AutomationEdge) and the firm's own Chrome extension can all fill V3 forms inside a session the staff member has logged into. That stays within the firm's rule as long as a person types the captcha and OTP and clicks submit. Hosted AI browser agents are a different matter. Skyvern openly sells built-in CAPTCHA solving and TOTP/2FA handling, which is prohibited here. Claude in Chrome's own policy forbids bypassing captchas and entering sensitive data. ChatGPT agent hands the login back to the human through "takeover". No source shows any vendor running a production bot on the MCA portal; the documented Indian cases are GST, TRACES and Income Tax.

### Inferences
- **Rating against the firm's rule ("nothing illegal"; no captcha/OTP bypass, no signing for another person, no borrowed login or DSC, no private-endpoint replay):**
  - *Firm's own Chrome extension (current prototype)*: **Allowed, lowest risk.** It runs in the staff member's own session, stores nothing, and a person does captcha, OTP and submit.
  - *Power Automate Desktop (attended), UiPath attended / Community, Automation Anywhere attended, AutomationEdge attended*: **Allowed if configured attended.** The bot must pause for the human at login, captcha, OTP and final submit, and the Windows credential vault or Orchestrator assets must not hold MCA passwords. Unattended licences are only useful with stored credentials and automated login, so they **should not be used** for MCA. These tools add little over the firm's extension apart from desktop steps such as moving files and signing-utility dialogs. Power Automate at about $15 per user per month is the cheapest licensed option.
  - *Playwright or Selenium "attended" bots*: **Allowed only if** they attach to the person's already-open browser or open a visible window and wait for the human at captcha and OTP. They must drive the visible UI rather than calling captured XHR endpoints, because replaying private endpoints is prohibited.
  - *Claude in Chrome*: **Allowed with conditions.** Its own policy forbids captcha bypass and sensitive-data entry, it works in the user's own Chrome, and Manually approve mode keeps a person in the loop. Anthropic's advice to keep government accounts out of the profile, and the user's responsibility for third-party terms, still mean the firm should treat it as experimental for MCA. Use a dedicated profile and manual approval, and never let it press final submit.
  - *ChatGPT agent / Operator*: **Higher risk.** The login is done by the human via takeover, which is good, but the MCA session lives in OpenAI's hosted browser and cookies persist there. That means client data and an authenticated government session sit with a third party, and the product's status is unclear.
  - *Skyvern (hosted or open source with solver on)*: **Prohibited as configured by default** because captcha solving and TOTP/SMS OTP capture are built-in selling points. Even with those turned off, credentials are stored in a vendor vault.
  - *TaxPido-type credential autofill extensions*: **Not recommended.** They store client passwords for portals, which the firm's design avoids. Using a client's password is "another person's login" unless the client has given written authority.
- Since no vendor has a documented MCA V3 bot, any RPA purchase would mean the firm builds and maintains the MCA flow itself on someone else's platform. The firm's extension already does this more cheaply.

### Gaps
- Not researched within the tool budget, so attended mode, credential storage and pricing are unverified: Blue Prism, Axiom.ai, Bardeen, Zapier/Make, browser-use, Browserbase, Multion, Magical, Text Blaze, Fillr, Kissflow, "Tally RPA". From background knowledge (unverified): Axiom.ai, Bardeen, Magical and Text Blaze are Chrome extensions that run in the user's own browser; Browserbase is hosted headless browser infrastructure that advertises captcha handling; browser-use is an open-source Python library. All of these need checking.
- No source confirms whether any vendor (Suvit, AutomationEdge, TaxPido or others) automates MCA V3 e-form filling specifically.
- The ICAI Playwright tool's captcha handling could not be confirmed because `ai.icai.org` is blocked in this session.
- MCA's own terms of use on automated access were not retrieved (`www.mca.gov.in` blocked). This is the key document for rating any bot.

## 2. DSC signing: how SPICe+ PDFs are signed, multi-signer remote tools, Aadhaar eSign, and re-saving/flattening risks

### Takeaway
SPICe+ on V3 is authenticated by DSC under Rule 8 of the Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules, 2014. Each subscriber and director signs with their own DSC through MCA's local signer bridge (emBridge), after registering as a Business User. No official source found says Aadhaar eSign is accepted for SPICe+; only vendor pages claim so. Third-party multi-signer platforms (Leegality, emSigner) do support USB-token DSC signing, but nothing shows MCA accepting a PDF signed outside its own signing flow. The safe pattern is that each signer signs the MCA-generated PDF themselves, on their own token, without anyone editing or re-saving the file after a signature is added.

### Cited Findings
- Rule 8(1) of the Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules, 2014 requires electronic forms to be authenticated by authorised signatories using a digital signature. A May 2025 amendment exists but its content was not retrieved. — [ca2013.com, Rule 8 (valid as on 05/08/2026)](https://ca2013.com/rule-8-companies-registration-offices-and-fees-rules-2014/)
- The completed SPICe+ form is converted to PDF, DSCs are affixed before final submission, and DSC validation happens at upload. — [taxguru, SPICe+ overview](https://taxguru.in/?p=718919); [kanakkupillai, SPICe+](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/new-learnwhat-is-spice-plus-form-in-company-incorporation/)
- All first subscribers and directors who must affix a DSC have to register as Business Users. Each person registers their own DSC, and one DSC cannot be shared between people. — [Razorpay Rize, SPICe+ FAQs](https://razorpay.com/rize/blogs/mca-spice-plus-faqs); [Patron Accounting, DSC for company registration](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/digital-signature-certificate-dsc-for-company-registration)
- The INC-9 declarations by subscribers and first directors are auto-generated as PDF. — [taxguru, SPICe+ overview](https://taxguru.in/?p=718919)
- DSC class conflicts between sources. One says Class 2 is enough for incorporation and ROC filing; others say MCA requires Class 3. Class 2 certificates were discontinued by CCA from 1 January 2021 (background; not re-verified here), so in practice new DSCs are Class 3. — [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/digital-signature-certificate-dsc-for-company-registration); contradicted by [Patron Accounting, DSC/DIN checker](https://www.patronaccounting.com/tools/dsc-din-requirement-checker)
- MCA V3 mainly uses a local signer utility, commonly called emBridge, which a practitioner guide calls "the single most common failure point". An outdated Class 2 DSC fails signature validation on V3. — [calcguru.in, MCA V3 errors and workarounds](https://calcguru.in/?p=9795); [legalsuvidha, DSC errors on MCA](https://legalsuvidha.com/blog/dsc-errors-on-mca-and-fixes)
- On older (V2-style) interactive MCA PDF forms, Adobe's "Digitally Sign" option was greyed out and Fill & Sign does not work on interactive forms. The MCA helpline advised Adobe Reader 11.0.10 (Adobe Community thread, older, V2 era). — [Adobe Community thread](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-reader-discussions/sign-document-option-is-quot-disabled-unavailable-greyed-out-quot-in-pdf-forms-and-files-unable-to/m-p/13038960)
- A PDF modified after a digital signature is applied shows an invalid signature. The fix is to go back to the signed original or re-sign after changes (general PDF signing behaviour). — [UPDF, PDF signature invalid](https://updf.com/fr/pdf-form/pdf-signature-invalid/)
- "At least one signature is invalid" in Adobe Reader is often a trust-settings display issue rather than a broken signature (vendor support, general). — [addosign support](https://support.addosign.com/en/at-least-one-signature-is-invalid-in-adobe-reader)
- Aadhaar eSign: eSign.AI vendor pages claim MCA accepts Aadhaar OTP eSign alongside DSC for SPICe+. No regulator source confirms this, and Rule 8 speaks of digital signatures by authorised signatories. — [eSign.AI blog (vendor)](https://www.esign.ai/blog/india-mca-esignature-for-new-company-registration)
- **Leegality**: supports DSC USB-token signing through a local "Leegality DSC utility" that needs a free local port (8009, 9800, 2048 or 3900). Each signatory uses their own token on a PC (token signing is PC only). One signer can bulk-sign up to 500 documents. Its "Doc Signer" alternative uses a cloud-stored PFX organisational certificate usable for multiple signatories. — [Leegality, DSC token signing journey](https://support.leegality.com/portal/en/kb/articles/dsc-token-signing-journey); [Leegality, bulk sign with DSC token](https://support.leegality.com/portal/kb/articles/procedure-for-bulk-sign-using-a-dsc-token); [Leegality blog, DSC tokens](https://www.leegality.com/blog/redefining-dsc-tokens-ft-leegality); [Leegality, DSC frequent issues](https://support.leegality.com/portal/en/kb/articles/dsc-esign-frequent-issues); [Leegality, Doc Signer](https://www.leegality.com/feature-explainers/doc-signer)
- **eMudhra emSigner**: lets a sender add several signers with a signing order. Signers get an emailed link and can be verified by face recognition or Aadhaar. It supports token-based DSC and remote signing, with keys in a server HSM where no physical token is required. eMudhra markets its DSCs (not emSigner workflows) for ROC filings and company registration. — [eMudhra, sign PDF with emSigner](https://emudhra.com/en/blog/digitally-sign-pdf-file-using-electronic-signature-software-emsigner); [emSigner brochure](https://resources.emudhra.com/CorporateDocs/emsigner/brochures/emSigner-brochure.pdf); [emSign, Remote Signing Made Simpler](https://emsign.com/resources/collaterals/Remote-Signing-Made-Simpler.pdf); [SourceForge, emSigner](https://sourceforge.net/software/product/emSigner)
- KDK Zen PDF says it works with all USB tokens and Class 2 and 3 DSCs from any licensed Indian CA. — [KDK Software, Zen PDF](https://www.kdksoftware.com/zen-pdf/)
- Capricorn sells a desktop "Signer Pro" product (listing only). — [G2, Capricorn Signer Pro](https://www.g2.com/products/capricorn-signer-pro/discuss)
- Adobe markets bulk digital signing for ROC users in India. — [Adobe India, bulk digital signatures](https://www.adobe.com/in/acrobat/roc/blog/bulk-digital-signatures.html)

### Inferences
- **Rating against the firm's rule:**
  - *MCA's own emBridge flow, each signer on their own token*: **Allowed, required baseline.**
  - *Remote signer signs the MCA PDF the firm emails*: **Allowed in principle.** Each signer is present with their own token and their own act of signing; this is how many practices work. The risk is technical, not legal. The firm must send the exact MCA-generated PDF, and signers must add a signature without editing, filling, flattening, printing to PDF or "Save As" in another tool, because any change after the first signature invalidates earlier ones. Signers should use the signing method MCA's help pages name (emBridge or the prescribed Adobe version), not a third-party platform that may rewrite the file.
  - *Leegality / emSigner token-based multi-signer workflows*: **Legal, but MCA acceptance unproven.** These platforms re-serve and may re-encode the PDF. Use them only for the firm's own attachments (board resolutions, consents, NOCs) that MCA accepts as signed attachments, not for the SPICe+ form PDF itself, until MCA acceptance is tested on a non-live sample.
  - *Cloud/HSM remote-signing certificates and organisational PFX ("Doc Signer")*: **Prohibited for SPICe+ signatures** whenever one certificate signs for several people, because each subscriber and director must sign with their own DSC. A person's own cloud DSC is acceptable only if MCA's signer accepts it, which was not confirmed.
  - *Firm staff holding clients' tokens and PINs and signing for them*: **Prohibited**, since the firm's rule forbids using another person's DSC without valid authority, and practitioner guidance says a DSC cannot be shared.
  - *Aadhaar eSign for SPICe+*: **Treat as not accepted** until an official MCA notice says otherwise. The only support is vendor marketing.

### Gaps
- The official MCA V3 user manual on emBridge, signing order, and whether all signatures go on one PDF or each signer signs in turn could not be read (`www.mca.gov.in` blocked). This is the most important open item.
- Not covered within the budget: Capricorn/Vsign (beyond a listing), Pantasign, Sify SafeScrypt, SignDesk, Signzy eSign, Docusign India with DSC. SignDesk's DSC flow returned no results.
- No source confirms whether the V3 SPICe+ PDF can be signed in Adobe at all, or only through emBridge.
- No source tested whether a PDF signed in Leegality or emSigner passes MCA upload validation.

## 3. Payment: automated or batched routes, MCA gateway options, NEFT/challan, and the 7-day rule

### Takeaway
After the SRN is generated, the DSC-signed SPICe+ PDF must be uploaded within 15 days and fees paid within 7 days of upload, or the SRN lapses and a fresh application is needed. These windows come from practitioner sources; no MCA circular was retrieved. V3 payment is online (net banking, cards, UPI and possibly NEFT). Offline "Pay Later" was stopped on V2 from 8 June 2025. No source describes an API, batch payment route or corporate bulk-payment facility for MCA fees, so payment remains a manual, per-SRN step done by a person.

### Cited Findings
- The DSC-affixed PDF must be uploaded within 15 days of SRN generation, and payment made within 7 days of a successful upload; failure cancels the SRN. — [Razorpay Rize, SPICe+ Part B](https://razorpay.com/rize/company-registration/document-required/spice-part-b); [compliancecalendar.in, PAS-3 instruction kit](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/resources/1714723834Instruction_Kit_PAS-3.pdf)
- Part B must be filed within 20 days of name approval (reserved name valid for 20 days). — [Razorpay Rize, SPICe+ FAQs](https://razorpay.com/rize/blogs/mca-spice-plus-faqs)
- A June 2026 consultancy guide lists V3 fee payment by net banking, NEFT, UPI and credit/debit card, with the SRN following payment. It does not mention challan or Pay Later. — [SPOTONZ, MCA V3 portal guide](https://www.spotonz.com/blog/mca-v3-portal-guide-india/)
- Offline payment through Pay Later on V2 was stopped from 8 June 2025, 12:00 AM; users were told to pay online by card or net banking. — [taxguru (search summary)](https://taxguru.in/?p=966094)
- On V2, modes included card, challan at an authorised bank, NEFT and net banking. Pay Later let a user generate an SRN and pay afterwards. Net banking carried no bank surcharge, while cards carried about 1% at some banks (older, V2 era). — [studycafe, FAQs on MCA payment](https://studycafe.in/making-payments-of-roc-fees-faqs-on-payment-process-of-mca-27746.html); [MCA V2 NEFT page](https://www.mca.gov.in/MinistryV2/neft.html); [corporatelawreporter, MCA NEFT payment](https://corporatelawreporter.com/payment-mca-neft/)
- One source says SPICe+ fees must be paid immediately with no Pay Later; another general eForm source mentions a Pay Later challan. This conflict was not resolved. — [Razorpay Rize](https://razorpay.com/rize/company-registration/document-required/spice-part-b); contradicted by [Quicko, upload eForms (search snippet)](https://learn.quicko.com/mca-portal-upload-eforms)

### Inferences
- The firm's app can automate the **reminders**, not the payment. It should record the SRN, the upload time and the payment deadline, and send email (and an optional WhatsApp nudge) before day 15 and day 7. This fits the existing compliance-calendar and notification design.
- Paying through an RPA or AI agent would mean the bot handles net banking or card credentials and bank OTPs. That is a bank-OTP bypass risk and conflicts with Claude in Chrome's financial-site restrictions (see section 1). **Rate: prohibited to automate**; a person pays.
- If NEFT on V3 works as it did on V2 (generate a challan, then pay from the firm's or client's corporate banking), the person can queue payments in bulk on the bank side. That is a legitimate way to "batch" the step, but it needs confirming on V3.

### Gaps
- No official MCA V3 page on payment modes, NEFT challan validity or the 7-day rule was retrieved (`www.mca.gov.in` blocked).
- It is unconfirmed whether NEFT challans exist on V3, and whether a challan's validity runs inside the 7-day window.
- No source on whether a third party (the firm) can pay on behalf of the applicant's SRN from its own account, or on refund handling.

## 4. Document generation for MOA/AOA and attachments, and PDF tools for MCA attachment limits

### Takeaway
MCA attachments must be PDF; a practitioner source puts the e-form/attachment cap at 6,400 KB (about 6.25 MB), with scanned papers converted to PDF. Very little sourced material was found on Docassemble, Docupilot, PandaDoc, Spellbook, pdf-lib, Adobe PDF Services or Apryse in the MCA context. The firm already generates docx in its own code (`src/lib/incorporation-docs/`, `src/lib/board-resolution/`), so third-party generators add little. The real need is a server-side step to convert to PDF, compress below the limit and check page and file size, done **before** any signature is applied.

### Cited Findings
- MCA forms, including SPICe+, must be DSC-signed before upload and kept under 6,400 KB; MCA rejects larger files. — [Quicko, MCA DSC settings (search snippet)](https://learn.quicko.com/mca-dsc-settings)
- Each uploaded file must be under 6 MB, per another practitioner source. — [kanakkupillai / patronaccounting search summary](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/digital-signature-certificate-dsc-for-company-registration)
- The MCA V2 system requirements page says scanned attachments must be converted to PDF with a PDF converter (V2 era; no size given). — [MCA, system requirements (V2)](https://www.mca.gov.in/MinistryV2/systemrequirements.html)
- In SPICe+, the INC-9 declarations are auto-generated as PDF, and e-MoA (INC-33) and e-AoA (INC-34) are linked web forms. — [taxguru, SPICe+ overview](https://taxguru.in/?p=718919); [spotonz, SPICe+ guide](https://www.spotonz.com/blog/spice-plus-company-incorporation-guide/)
- A 2026 commentary describes MCA pushing toward digital-only e-filing without physical documents. — [incorpx.io, digital-only MCA (2026)](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/mca-e-filing-without-physical-documents-2026)
- Common MCA V3 filing failures and workarounds (including signer-utility failures) are collected in a practitioner guide. — [calcguru.in, MCA V3 errors](https://calcguru.in/?p=9795); [studycafe, technical issues on MCA e-forms](https://studycafe.in/solution-to-technical-issues-faced-during-filing-of-e-forms-on-mca-portal-123149.html)

### Inferences
- Because e-MoA and e-AoA are web forms on V3, an MOA/AOA document generator mainly matters for attachments (subscriber sheets, NOCs, board resolutions, consents) and for any non-standard articles. The firm's existing docx pipeline covers these, so buying Docassemble, Docupilot, PandaDoc or Spellbook duplicates built work (and the house rule is reuse first).
- A PDF step (pdf-lib in Node, or a hosted service such as Adobe PDF Services or Apryse) belongs in an Inngest job: convert, downsample scans, keep files under about 6 MB, and do not touch a file once anyone has signed it. **Rating: allowed, low risk.** The main risk is data residency when client KYC goes to a foreign cloud PDF API; a local library avoids it.

## 5. Captcha and OTP: services marketed for solving or forwarding, marked PROHIBITED

### Takeaway
Any captcha-solving service, or any feature that captures OTPs automatically, is **prohibited** for this firm. A captcha and OTP are the portal owner's controls that a human is present and authorised. Defeating them with a machine risks "access without permission" under sections 43 and 66 of the IT Act, 2000, which government portals cite in their warnings. It also breaks the firm's own rule, and AI-agent vendors like Anthropic forbid it in their own policy. The legal question is not settled in case law, which is a reason to stay clear rather than a licence.

### Cited Findings
- Section 43(a) of the IT Act penalises anyone who, without the owner's permission, accesses or secures access to a computer system or network. Government portals warn against unauthorised access citing sections 43 and 66 (DigiLocker sign-in page). — [conventuslaw, cybersecurity laws India 2025](https://conventuslaw.com/report/cybersecurity-laws-and-regulations-india-2025/); [DigiLocker sign-in](https://accounts.digilocker.gov.in/signin/smart_v2)
- One commentary notes that section 43 cannot be invoked where the person was authorised to do the act, so the question turns on whether the automated access is permitted. Another says confidence is low because there is no judicial precedent and the law is changing. — [law.ungovr.org, AI law India](https://law.ungovr.org/ai/in); [conventuslaw](https://conventuslaw.com/report/cybersecurity-laws-and-regulations-india-2025/)
- Vendor and low-reliability pages say captcha solving is not illegal in itself. These are self-interested sources. — [geetest blog, captcha solving](https://blog.geetest.com/en/article/captcha-solving); [sharepresentation, captcha bypass API India](https://www.sharepresentation.com/azapiaiservices1071/free-captcha-bypass-api-extensions-india)
- India has a long-standing human captcha-solving industry that sells solving to bulk automation (older reporting, around 2008 to 2009). — [Computerworld, India's booming business in captcha solving](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1356522/india-s-booming-business-captcha-solving.html); [MediaNama, captcha entry operators (2008)](https://www.medianama.com/2008/09/223-indias-captcha-entry-operators-they-make-money-from-spamming-our-sites/)
- How captcha solvers work, using human farms or machine-learning models through an API. — [TechRadar, what is a CAPTCHA solver](https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/what-is-a-captcha-solver-and-how-does-it-work)
- Skyvern sells native captcha solving, 2FA/TOTP handling and an SMS-number add-on for receiving OTPs. — [Skyvern pricing](https://skyvern.com/pricing)
- Anthropic's Claude in Chrome lists "Bypassing captchas" as a prohibited action. — [Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12902428-using-claude-in-chrome-safely)

### Inferences
- **PROHIBITED list for the firm (with reason):**
  - *2Captcha, Anti-Captcha, CapSolver and similar solving APIs or extensions*: prohibited. They defeat MCA's human-presence control, which is the "captcha bypass" the firm bans, and they create section 43/66 exposure for access the portal owner has not allowed. The captcha image also goes to an unknown third party.
  - *Built-in solvers in agent platforms (Skyvern's basic and advanced solver, and any similar "stealth" or "auto-captcha" feature in hosted browser platforms)*: prohibited. Turn them off or do not use the platform for MCA.
  - *OTP forwarding (SMS-forwarding apps, virtual numbers such as Skyvern's SMS add-on, email-to-webhook rules that pipe MCA OTPs into a bot, stored TOTP seeds)*: prohibited. The OTP proves the registered user is present; routing a client's or director's OTP to the firm's machine amounts to using their login without them. Where the OTP goes to the staff member's own phone, the staff member types it by hand.
  - *Password vaults that auto-log into a client's MCA account (TaxPido-style)*: not a bypass, but they hold another person's login. Allowed only with the client's written authority, and the firm's no-credentials design is safer.
- The firm's Chrome extension, which stops at the login and captcha and resumes only after a person signs in, matches the rule. A useful control is for the extension to detect captcha or OTP fields and refuse to fill them, with a log entry, so no future change can add a solver quietly.
