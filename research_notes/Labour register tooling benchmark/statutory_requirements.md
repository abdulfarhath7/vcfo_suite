## Q1. The register regime today: which Acts mandate which registers for a ~400-person IT/services company in Telangana, Karnataka, Kerala and Haryana

### Takeaway
As of November 2026 the regime is **in transition and genuinely bifurcated**: the four Labour Codes
commenced on 21 November 2025 and the **Central Rules under all four were notified on 8 May 2026**,
replacing the central register sets (Code on Wages Forms I/IV/IX; OSH Forms XIII/XIV/XV) — but the
**state** Shops & Establishments registers for Telangana, Karnataka, Kerala and Haryana remain on the
old state rules because none of those four states had final rules under all four Codes as at late
October 2026. A 400-person IT/services firm is a "shop/commercial establishment", not a factory, so
the Factories Act 1948 is generally irrelevant; CLRA and ISMW bite only on contractor-supplied labour.

### Cited Findings
- The four Codes (Code on Wages 2019, Industrial Relations Code 2020, Code on Social Security 2020, OSH&WC Code 2020) were **made effective 21 November 2025**, consolidating/replacing 29 central labour laws — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/centre-brings-four-labour-codes-into-operation-with-effect-from-november-21-310772); [K&S "All Four Labour Codes Enforced from 21 November 2025"](https://ksandk.com/labour-employment/all-four-labour-codes-enforced-from-21-november-2025/); [KPMG Flash Alert 2025-267](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/flash-alert-2025-267.html)
- **Central Rules under all four Codes were notified on 8 May 2026.** The Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026 carries gazette reference `CG-DL-E-08052026-272365` — [Gazette Tracker](https://gazettetracker.com/g/CG-DL-E-08052026-272365); [Taxmann analysis of Wages (Central) Rules 2026](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/wages-central-rules-2026-code-on-wages-chapter-wise-guide); [Legal500 — Notified Code on Wages (Central) Rules 2026](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/employment-and-hr/notified-code-on-wages-central-rules-2026-what-employers-need-to-know)
- **Code on Wages (Central) Rules 2026, rule 51** requires three registers, maintainable "electronically or in physical form in the formats appended to these rules": **Form I — Employee Register; Form IV — Register of Wages, Overtime, Advances, Fines and Deductions for Damage and Loss; Form IX — Attendance Register-cum-Muster Roll** — [Lawspedia, rule 51 text](https://www.lawspedia.in/rule-51-of-the-code-on-wages-central-rules-2026)
- **OSH&WC (Central) Rules 2026, rule 72** requires: **Form XIII — employee register; Form XIV — attendance register-cum-muster roll; Form XV — register of wages, overtime and deductions**; registers "shall be maintained electronically or otherwise" and must be produced on demand before the Inspector-cum-Facilitator. The accident register is reported at **Form XIX** (rule 75). Gazette cited as **G.S.R. 345(E), 8 May 2026** — [Lawspedia rule 72](https://www.lawspedia.in/rule-72-of-the-occupational-safety-health-and-working-conditions-central-rules-2026); [Lawspedia OSH (Central) Rules 2026 index](https://www.lawspedia.in/category/labour-laws/the-occupational-safety-health-and-working-conditions-central-rules-2026); [iPleaders OSH Central Rules 2026](https://blog.ipleaders.in/osh-central-rules-2026-registration-hours-contract-labour/)
- **Telangana Shops and Establishments Rules, 1990, rule 29**: Form XXII — Register of Employment (r.29(1)); Form XXIII — Register of Wages (r.29(2)); Form XXV — Register of Leave (r.29(6)) — [greytHR Form XXII](https://www.greythr.com/wiki/compliances/telangana-shops-and-establishments-act-form-xxii-register-of-employment/); [Form XXIII](https://www.greythr.com/wiki/compliances/telangana-shops-and-establishments-act-form-xxiii-register-of-wages/); [Form XXV](https://www.greythr.com/wiki/compliances/telangana-shops-and-establishments-act-form-xxv-register-of-leave/); rules text at [labour.telangana.gov.in — Telangana S&E Rules 1990](https://labour.telangana.gov.in/content/links/THE%20TELANGANA%20SHOPS%20&%20ESTABLISHMENT%20RULES%201990.htm) and [Telangana S&E Rules 1990 PDF](https://www.datocms-assets.com/40521/1638956847-telangana-shops-and-establishments-rules-1990.pdf)
- **Conflict on Telangana:** the Labour Department downloads page reportedly lists "Form XIII – Register of Wages", which conflicts with the rule text (Form XXIII); and one 2026 vendor guide describes Form XXII as covering "Employment, Wages & Overtime". The rule text (XXII employment / XXIII wages / XXV leave) is the better reading — [greytHR Telangana S&E Act overview](https://www.greythr.com/wiki/acts/telangana-shops-commercial-establishments-act/); [labour.telangana.gov.in checklist page](https://labour.telangana.gov.in/Checklist.do)
- **Karnataka Shops and Commercial Establishments Rules, 1963**: **Form T** is the combined **muster roll and register of wages** (includes overtime particulars); **Form F** is the **Leave with Wages Register**; **Form H** is the **leave-with-wages book / leave record issued to the employee**. Registration application is Form A; registration certificate Form C — [Karnataka S&CE Rules 1963 PDF](https://www.comply360.in/labor-law-library/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/THE-KARNATAKA-SHOPS-AND-COMMERCIAL-ESTABLISHMENTS-RULES-1963-.pdf); [keka Karnataka S&E guide](https://www.keka.com/compliance/acts/karnataka-shops-and-establishments-act); [greytHR Karnataka S&CE Act](https://www.greythr.com/wiki/acts/karnataka-shops-commercial-establishments-act/)
- **Kerala (Shops and Commercial Establishments Act, 1960 / Rules 1961)**: the register set found is **Form A or B — employment register; Form BB — service record; Form F — register of holidays and leave**. **No source confirmed a Kerala "Form XIV" register** — the "XIV" that appears in the Act document is a *section/chapter heading*, not a form — [Kerala S&CE Act 1960 PDF](https://banyanhr.com/DOWNLOADS/LABOUR%20LAW/SHOPS%20AND%20COMMERCIAL%20ESTABLISHMENTS%20ACT%201960%20KERALA.pdf)
- **Haryana (Shops and Commercial Establishments Act, 1958 / Rules 1958)**: the wages register is reported as **Form D** (wages fixed, arrears from last month, wages earned, overtime, wages due, deductions shown in Register E, advances, payments made), with deductions in **Form E**. **No source calls the Haryana wages register "Form XIII".** The "Form XIII" and "Form XIV" hits are **central CLRA** forms (Form XIII = register of workmen employed by contractor; Form XIV = employment card) — [Deskera Haryana S&CE guide](https://www.deskera.com/blog/haryana-shops-commercial-establishments-act/); [iPleaders Haryana Shops & Establishment Act](https://blog.ipleaders.in/haryana-shops-and-establishment-act/); [greytHR CLRA Form XIII](https://www.greythr.com/wiki/compliances/central-register-workmen-employer-contractor-form-xiii/)
- **Minimum Wages (Central) Rules, 1950** (the pattern copied by state MW rules): **rule 26(5)** muster roll kept at the work-spot with attendance recorded daily within three hours of shift start — originally **Form V**, later amended to point at **"Form D specified in the Schedule to the Ease of Compliance to Maintain Registers under various Labour Laws Rules, 2017"**; **Form X** = register of wages at the work-spot; **Form XI** = wage slip, to be issued "at least a day prior to the disbursement of wages" — [Minimum Wages (Central) Rules 1950, indiankanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/130387733/); [MW (Central) Rules 1950 PDF](https://www.comply360.in/labor-law-library/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/THE-MINIMUM-WAGES-CENTRAL-RULES-1950-1-1.pdf)
- **CLRA (Central) Rules, 1971**: contractor registers/records; **rule 79** = display of abstract of Act and rules (substituted by G.S.R. 1594(E) of 29 December 2017); **rule 80** = registers to be kept complete and up to date — [CLRA Central Rules 1971 PDF](https://www.comply360.in/labor-law-library/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/The-Contract-Labour-Regulation-and-Abolition-Central-Rules-1971.pdf)
- **Payment of Bonus**: **Form C** is the statement of bonus paid to employees for the accounting year — [Form list, slideshare compliance forms](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/compliance-forms-under-important-labour-lawsspdf/259113272); [advocatekhoj labour law forms](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/legalforms/formsfor/index.php?Fno=labourlaw.php)
- **Equal Remuneration Act 1976**: **Form D** register under s.8 of the Act read with rule 6 of the Equal Remuneration Rules 1976 — remuneration details component-wise and consolidated, with workers' information gender-wise — [greytHR Form D](https://www.greythr.com/wiki/compliances/central-form-d-register-be-maintained-employer/)
- There is an official **Ministry of Labour "Compliance Handbook for Employers Under the Four Labour Codes" (uploaded Feb 2026)** at [labour.gov.in handbook PDF](https://www.labour.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/83978455025732b99b0165def80ab171.pdf) — it states that under the new Codes "the record shall be preserved for period of 5 years". **This is the single most important primary-ish document for the product and could not be opened in this session.**

### Inferences
- For a 400-headcount IT/services establishment the live register set is, in practice, **two stacks**: (a) the **central** Code/Code-rules stack (Wages Forms I/IV/IX; OSH Forms XIII/XIV/XV, with XIII/XIV/XV substantially duplicating I/IX/IV), and (b) the **state Shops & Establishments** stack (Telangana XXII/XXIII/XXV; Karnataka T/F/H; Kerala A-or-B/BB/F; Haryana D/E plus its employment and leave registers). A generator must model both and let the state stack be switched off per state as and when that state notifies Code rules.
- **Factories Act 1948 is almost certainly out of scope** for a pure IT/services employer (no manufacturing process), so Forms 12/14/15 etc. need not be generated unless the client has a plant. No source was found suggesting otherwise.
- **CLRA and ISMW registers are the *contractor's* obligation** with the principal employer holding Form XII (register of contractors) and verification duties. For an IT/services firm with housekeeping/security/facilities vendors this is the realistic exposure; the product should generate the principal-employer-side register and a contractor-attestation workflow rather than the contractor's own Forms XIII–XIX.
- **Maternity Benefit Act registers survive the Codes only to the extent the Social Security Code rules re-prescribe them** — the SS Code repealed the MB Act 1961 from 21 Nov 2025 on the same section-69-style repeal pattern as the ERA. Not verified.

### Gaps
- **Kerala "Form XIV" could not be verified at all.** The brief's premise (Kerala Form XIV) was not corroborated by any source found; the Kerala register appears to be Form A/B + BB + F. Needs the consolidated Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments Rules, 1961 from lc.kerala.gov.in.
- **Haryana "Form XIII" could not be verified.** Sources point to Form D (wages) and Form E (deductions). Needs hrylabour.gov.in rules text.
- The exact current Telangana form numbering (XXIII vs the portal's "XIII") is unresolved.
- Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act registers: no specific finding was obtained in this session. The ISMW Act is listed among the nine Acts covered by the 2017 Ease of Compliance Rules (see Q2) and among the 29 Acts subsumed by the Codes, but no form-level detail was retrieved.
- Payment of Wages Act, Payment of Bonus Act and Maternity Benefit Act register form numbers beyond Bonus Form C were not retrieved form-by-form.
- No source was found confirming or denying whether a 400-person IT/services establishment in these states crosses any threshold that adds registers (e.g. crèche, canteen, standing orders) — Karnataka/Telangana IT-sector S&E exemption notifications (common for IT/ITES on working hours and women's night work, usually conditional on registers being maintained) were **not** located and are a significant known unknown.

## Q2. The Ease of Compliance to Maintain Registers under Various Labour Laws Rules, 2017 — the five Forms, the nine Acts, and whether they replace or sit alongside state registers

### Takeaway
The 2017 Rules consolidated ~56 registers under **nine central Acts** into **five combined Forms A–E**,
and expressly permit electronic maintenance. Critically, they operated by **amending the central rules
under each of those nine Acts to substitute the new Forms** — so centrally they *replace*; but for Acts
administered through **state** rules they do **not** displace the state format, which continues until the
state government amends its own rules. Several states (e.g. Gujarat) have since done so with their own
mirror rules. **Note the sources conflict on the gazette number: G.S.R. 154(E) vs G.S.R. 1048(E).**

### Cited Findings
- Notified by the Ministry of Labour and Employment and **effective 21 February 2017**. One source gives the number as **G.S.R. 154(E)**, another as **G.S.R. 1048(E)** — [TaxGuru short note on Ease of Compliance Rules 2017](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/short-note-ease-compliance-rule-2017.html); [ALMT Legal note PDF](https://almtlegal.com/articles-pdf/The%20Ease%20of%20Compliance%20to%20Maintain%20Registers%20under%20various%20Labour%20Laws%20Rules%20Notified.pdf); [Legally India](https://www.legallyindia.com/views/entry/the-ease-of-compliance-to-maintain-registers-under-various-labour-laws-rules-notified/)
- **The five Forms: Form A — Employee Register; Form B — Wage Register; Form C — Register of Loans/Recoveries; Form D — Attendance Register; Form E — Register of Rest Days / Leave / Leave Wages.** Form A has a **Part A for all establishments and a Part B for the Mines Act only** — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/short-note-ease-compliance-rule-2017.html); [TaxHeal tag page](https://www.taxheal.com/tag/ease-of-compliance-to-maintain-registers-under-various-labour-laws-rules-2017); [ComplianceBook](https://compliancebook.in/ease-of-compliance-rules-2017/)
- They **replace 56 separate registers mandated under nine central labour laws with five simplified registers** — [HRStop, digital compliance and online records](https://www.hrstop.com/Content/489/digital-compliance-and-online-records); [greytHR, labour law registers and forms modified](https://www.greythr.com/blog/labour-law-registers-and-forms-modified-easier-compliance/)
- **The nine Acts** (per a Karnataka Employers' Association circular): Building and Other Construction Workers (RE&CS) Act 1996; Contract Labour (R&A) Act 1970; Equal Remuneration Act 1976; Inter-State Migrant Workmen (RE&CS) Act 1979; Mines Act 1952; Minimum Wages Act 1948; Payment of Wages Act 1936; Sales Promotion Employees (Conditions of Service) Act 1976; Working Journalists and other Newspaper Employees (Conditions of Service) Act 1955 — [KEA Circular 29/2017 PDF](https://kea.co.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/circular-29-2017-EASE-OF-COMPLIANCE-OF-LABOUR-LAW-RULES.pdf). **One other summary lists only six Acts**, so the list needs gazette confirmation — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/short-note-ease-compliance-rule-2017.html)
- **The amendment mechanism (this is the decisive evidence):** the Minimum Wages (Central) Rules 1950 text as amended now directs the rule 26(5) muster roll to be kept **"in Form D specified in the Schedule to the Ease of Compliance to Maintain Registers under various Labour Laws Rules, 2017"** — i.e. the 2017 Rules were given effect by substituting forms inside each parent central rule set — [Minimum Wages (Central) Rules 1950, indiankanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/130387733/)
- CLRA (Central) Rules 1971 **rule 79 was substituted by G.S.R. 1594(E) dated 29 December 2017**, consistent with the same 2017 consolidation exercise reaching into the parent rules — [CLRA Central Rules 1971 PDF](https://www.comply360.in/labor-law-library/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/The-Contract-Labour-Regulation-and-Abolition-Central-Rules-1971.pdf)
- **Position of state rules:** "Where State Rules apply, the existing format may be used until the respective State Government amends it." State governments "are required to amend their rules under the Specified Labour Laws in line with the Ease of Compliance Rules" — [Lexology / Mondaq note, new rules for maintaining combined registers](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=61c16eae-db66-4fa8-ad34-55c19bbe8a38); [Mondaq duplicate](https://www.mondaq.com/india/employee-rights-labour-relations/573792/new-rules-for-maintaining-combined-registers-under-various-specified-labour-laws)
- **Gujarat example:** Gujarat removed separate registers and introduced combined registers by amending five state rule sets, via its own *Ease of Compliance to Maintain Registers under various Labour Laws (Gujarat) Rules, 2017* — [Simpliance, Gujarat Ease of Compliance Rules 2017](https://www.simpliance.in/India/LEI/govt_notification/Gujarat/the-ease-of-compliance-to-maintain-registers-under-various-labour-laws-gujarat-rules-2017-300). A Tripura draft of 2019 followed an A–C structure — [Simpliance central notification page](https://www.simpliance.in/India/LEI/govt_notification/Central/ease-of-compliance-to-maintain-registers-under-various-labour-law-rules-2017-92)
- **Electronic maintenance is permitted:** "Employers may keep these registers in electronic or physical form" / the Rules "allow an employer to maintain the combined registers in electronic form" — [AZB note](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=1410); [ALMT Legal PDF](https://almtlegal.com/articles-pdf/The%20Ease%20of%20Compliance%20to%20Maintain%20Registers%20under%20various%20Labour%20Laws%20Rules%20Notified.pdf); [Agama Law](https://agamalaw.in/2017/04/04/maintenance-of-register-under-various-labour-laws/)
- **Post-Code treatment:** advisories note that rules framed under the 29 repealed central labour laws "shall be deemed to be framed under the Codes" pending new rules — [Lexology note](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=61c16eae-db66-4fa8-ad34-55c19bbe8a38)

### Inferences
- **Answer to the product question: neither 12 state forms per state nor 5 combined forms alone — you need both, keyed by Act-administration.** The 2017 Forms A–E replace the *central* registers under the nine Acts (Minimum Wages, Payment of Wages, CLRA, ERA, ISMW, BOCW, Mines, Sales Promotion Employees, Working Journalists) **for centrally-administered establishments**. They do **not** touch state Shops & Establishments Acts at all — S&E Acts are not among the nine. So Telangana XXII/XXIII/XXV, Karnataka T/F/H etc. are untouched by the 2017 Rules and must still be generated.
- Because the 2017 Rules consolidated by *substituting forms in parent rules*, and the Codes now **repeal those parent Acts**, the 2017 Forms A–E are functionally **superseded for central purposes by the 2026 Central Rules forms** (Wages I/IV/IX, OSH XIII/XIV/XV) wherever the Codes and their rules are operative. Forms A–E remain relevant only for (i) states still on pre-Code state rules that adopted A–E, and (ii) the transition/deemed-rules window.
- The product's data model should therefore be **one canonical employee/wage/attendance/leave/deduction dataset with pluggable form renderers**, not form-shaped storage. Forms A–E, Wages I/IV/IX, OSH XIII/XIV/XV and the state S&E forms are all projections of the same underlying fields.

### Gaps
- **The operative text on electronic registers and on state rules could not be quoted verbatim** — the gazette PDF and the bare-act pages were unreachable. The brief asked for a quote; it must be obtained from the gazette notification before product copy or client-facing compliance claims are written.
- The gazette number conflict (G.S.R. 154(E) vs 1048(E)) is unresolved.
- Whether Telangana, Karnataka, Kerala or Haryana ever notified their own mirror "Ease of Compliance (State) Rules" (as Gujarat did) was **not established**. This is directly material and should be the first follow-up.
- No source stated a retention period inside the 2017 Rules themselves.

## Q3. The four Labour Codes — commencement status as at November 2026, rules notified, and what they say about registers

### Takeaway
Yes: all four Codes were brought into force **21 November 2025**, and **Central Rules under all four
were notified 8 May 2026**. What is operative centrally is therefore substantially complete. What is
**not** complete is the states: as at **28 October 2026** only Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh
had final rules under all four Codes, with **Karnataka, Telangana and Haryana still at draft stage and
Kerala showing nothing newer than a 2021 draft**. Registers under the central rules are a **short
electronic-or-physical set (three forms per Code, with an express deeming rule so the sets do not
duplicate)**, not literally a "single register". **"Form XVI" is not a Code on Wages register** — the
number appears in OSH-rules and old CLRA numbering and sources conflict about it.

### Cited Findings
- Commencement **21 November 2025** for all four Codes; the Gazette of India Extraordinary Part II of that date carries the commencement notifications appointing that date for specified provisions — [KPMG Flash Alert 2025-267](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/flash-alert-2025-267.html); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/centre-brings-four-labour-codes-into-operation-with-effect-from-november-21-310772); [Maharashtra Labour Dept, implementation of labour codes PDF](https://labour.maharashtra.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-11/implementation-of-labour-codes.pdf)

- **OSH (Central) Rules rule 72(3) deeming provision:** registers already kept under the Code on Wages rules are **deemed to be maintained under the OSH rules**, so no duplicate set is needed — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/osh-central-rules-2026-registration-hours-contract-labour/); [LexComply, OSH (Central) Rules 2026 unified framework](https://lexcomply.com/blog/osh-central-rules-2026-unified-occupational-safety-compliance-framework-6a01be57aa0de)
- **Annual return:** an electronic annual return in **Form XVII** by the **last day of February** for the previous calendar year (OSH rules); Code on Wages rule 48 returns are filed electronically in the forms under the OSH Code — [LexComply registers and returns matrix](https://lexcomply.com/feeds/employer-registers-returns-matrix); [Taxmann Wages (Central) Rules 2026 guide](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/wages-central-rules-2026-code-on-wages-chapter-wise-guide)
- **"Form XVI" — sources conflict.** One calls it the **wage slip** under the 2026 Central Rules, filed electronically on or before the day of payment; another says the wage slip is **Form V** under the Code on Wages rules and **Form XVI** under the OSH rules; a third (older, CLRA numbering) calls Form XVI the **muster roll** with Form XVIII the wages-cum-muster roll — [HRMantra wage slip guide](https://www.hrmantra.com/guide/salary-slip-format-2026-labour-codes-india.html); [LexComply matrix](https://lexcomply.com/feeds/employer-registers-returns-matrix); [greytHR CLRA Form XVII](https://www.greythr.com/wiki/compliances/central-register-wages-form-xvii/)
- **"One registration, one set of registers, one annual return"** is commentary describing the overall simplification (replacing ~15 older central rule sets), **not the wording of rule 72** — the rule itself keeps three forms — [LexComply OSH blog](https://lexcomply.com/blog/osh-central-rules-2026-unified-occupational-safety-compliance-framework-6a01be57aa0de); [ZipLaw OSH Central Rules 2026](https://ziplaw.in/occupational-safety-health-and-working-conditions-central-rules-2026/)
- **PIB release** on the Code on Wages framing — [PIB PRID 2193095](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2193095&reg=3&lang=2)

### Inferences
- **Product consequence:** between now and whenever Karnataka/Telangana/Haryana/Kerala notify final Code rules, a client in those states is on a **hybrid**: central-rule forms where the central Codes/rules operate, plus the unamended state S&E registers. The generator needs a **per-state, per-date "regime" switch** and must be able to produce both sets for the same month. Hard-coding a single "2026 regime" will produce wrong output for these four states.
- Because rule 72(3) deems Code-on-Wages registers to satisfy the OSH rules, the economical output is **Wages Forms I/IV/IX plus the OSH-only extras (accident register Form XIX, annual return Form XVII)** — not six near-duplicate forms.
- **"Form XVI" should not be implemented** until the gazette is read. The evidence is contradictory and at least one reading (CLRA muster roll) is plainly legacy numbering.

### Gaps
- **No gazette PDF was opened.** The commencement notifications, the Code on Wages (Central) Rules 2026 and OSH (Central) Rules 2026 texts are all unverified at source here.
- **Which specific provisions did NOT commence on 21 November 2025** could not be enumerated. Sources say "certain provisions" remain; none listed them.
- Status of **Industrial Relations (Central) Rules 2026** and **Social Security (Central) Rules 2026** register/record provisions was not retrieved (only Wages and OSH were).
- No source gave a date by which Karnataka, Telangana, Kerala or Haryana are expected to finalise rules.
- Whether the old Acts' registers are legally safe-harboured during the transition (a "deemed compliance" provision) was asserted by one advisory only and is unverified.

## Q4. Electronic maintenance and signing

### Takeaway
Electronic maintenance is clearly permitted and is now the default assumption under both the 2017 Rules
and the 2026 Central Rules. The key practical constraint is the long-standing statutory requirement
that **a printout be made available to the Inspector on demand**. No source established a required
**class** of digital signature for labour registers; the general IT Act 2000 position is that electronic
signatures are legally equivalent, but the only labour/social-security context where DSC use is
*expressly* permitted that was found is **EPF filings**. **No source confirmed a local-language
(Kannada/Malayalam/Telugu) requirement for the registers themselves** — Karnataka's Kannada mandate
found relates to **name boards, notices and the abstract of the Act**, not the registers.

### Cited Findings
- **The Labour Laws (Exemption from Furnishing Returns and Maintaining Registers by Certain Establishments) Act** as amended in 2014 permits registers/records on "computer, computer floppy, diskette or other electronic form", **provided that "a printout of such returns, registers, books and records or a portion thereof is made available to the Inspector on demand"** — [Labour Laws (Amendment) Act 2014 PDF, PRS India](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2014/the-labour-laws-(amendment)-act,-2014.pdf)
- The 2017 Ease of Compliance Rules allow combined registers in electronic form — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=1410); [ALMT Legal](https://almtlegal.com/articles-pdf/The%20Ease%20of%20Compliance%20to%20Maintain%20Registers%20under%20various%20Labour%20Laws%20Rules%20Notified.pdf)
- **Code on Wages (Central) Rules 2026 rule 51:** registers maintained "electronically or in physical form in the formats appended to these rules" — [Lawspedia rule 51](https://www.lawspedia.in/rule-51-of-the-code-on-wages-central-rules-2026)
- **OSH (Central) Rules 2026 rule 72:** registers "shall be maintained electronically or otherwise", and the employer must **produce them on demand before the Inspector-cum-Facilitator** — [Lawspedia rule 72](https://www.lawspedia.in/rule-72-of-the-occupational-safety-health-and-working-conditions-central-rules-2026)
- Under the Codes "maintenance of registers in electronic form [is allowed], provided they are accessible and accurate" — [UnsolvedLegal, statutory registers under the new labour codes](https://unsolvedlegal.com/blog/statutory-registers-under-new-labour-codes/)
- **Contradicted:** one commercial compliance blog asserts "electronic registers are compulsory". That claim is **not supported by any statutory text found** and should be treated as wrong — [ZingHR labour law compliance checklist 2026](https://www.zinghr.com/blogs/labour-law-compliance-checklist)
- **IT Act 2000** gives electronic signatures legal standing "at par with handwritten signatures"; **Aadhaar eSign** is valid where operated under the Second Schedule of the IT Act and the Controller's guidelines — [eSignGlobal](https://www.esignglobal.com/blog/are-electronic-signatures-legal-in-india); [TrueCopy](https://truecopy.in/blog/digital-signatures-in-india-are-they-legally-binding/); [ESign (India), Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESign_(India))
- On statutory forms specifically: "only PF authorities have explicitly allowed the use of digital signatures to submit certain statutory forms" — [Alldigi Tech, are employee e-signatures valid](https://www.alldigitech.com/2022/07/21/are-employee-e-signatures-valid/)
- **Karnataka language requirement (not registers):** "the name Board of every establishment shall be in Kannada"; other languages may appear only below the Kannada version; **notices and extracts of the Act** must be in Kannada and in the language of the majority of employees — [Karnataka S&CE Rules 1963 PDF](https://www.comply360.in/labor-law-library/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/THE-KARNATAKA-SHOPS-AND-COMMERCIAL-ESTABLISHMENTS-RULES-1963-.pdf); [Abstract under Karnataka S&CE Rules 1963](https://www.comply360.in/labor-law-library/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Abstract-Under-Karnataka-Shops-And-Commercial-Establishment-Rules-1963.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Product design conclusion:** generate a **PDF print bundle** as a first-class output, not an afterthought. The statutory hook for electronic maintenance is conditional on producing a printout on demand, and both the 2026 central rules frame the obligation as "produce before the Inspector-cum-Facilitator". A per-register, per-period, paginated PDF with a signature block is the inspection artefact.
- **Do not build a mandatory DSC/eSign flow.** No labour rule found prescribes a signature class for registers. Safest design: an authorised-signatory attestation captured in-app (name, designation, date, audit trail), plus an optional DSC/Aadhaar-eSign slot, plus a wet-signature print line — and let the firm decide per client. A wet signature on the printout remains the lowest-risk default for inspection.
- **Bilingual output is probably not required for the registers**, but **the abstract/notice artefacts almost certainly are** in Karnataka (Kannada) and plausibly Kerala (Malayalam) and Telangana (Telugu). If the product also generates the statutory abstract/notice, that is where bilingual rendering is needed. Treat register bilingualism as a nice-to-have pending state confirmation.

### Gaps
- **No source found any state that still insists on bound paper registers.** Absence of evidence, not evidence of absence — this needs the four state rules read directly (older S&E rules sometimes require registers to be "bound" and pages "serially numbered", which would be fatal to a pure-electronic claim; this was not verified either way).
- **No source stated a required digital signature class** (Class 2/Class 3) for labour registers.
- **Nothing was found on what the Shram Suvidha portal itself says about electronic registers** — the portal's own guidance page was not reachable.
- Whether registers themselves must be in the local language in Kerala, Telangana or Haryana was **not established**.

## Q5. Retention periods

### Takeaway
The old-Act pattern is **three years** (Minimum Wages rule 26A; CLRA Central Rules rule 80 — "three
calendar years from the date of last entry"). The Codes move this to **five years** (Code on Wages
(Central) Rules rule 51; OSH (Central) Rules rule 72 — "five calendar years from the date of last
entry"), confirmed by the Ministry's own 2026 compliance handbook. **The commonly-cited "eight years"
could not be verified from any source** and should not be relied on.

### Cited Findings
- **Minimum Wages (Central) Rules 1950, rule 26A:** registers "and the muster-roll required to be maintained under rule 26(5) shall be preserved for a period of **three years** after the date of last entry made therein" — rule inserted by **G.S.R. 1060 dated 3.9.1960** and the muster-roll words substituted by **G.S.R. 1523 dated 16.12.1960** — [Minimum Wages (Central) Rules 1950, indiankanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/130387733/)
- The same three-year rule appears in state copies, e.g. the **Assam Minimum Wages Rules 1952** — [Assam MW Rules PDF](https://labourcommissioner.assam.gov.in/sites/default/files/public_utility/the_minimum_wages_rules_1952.pdf); and the pattern is repeated in the Maharashtra MW Rules 1963 and Gujarat MW Rules 1961 — [Maharashtra MW Rules 1963 PDF](https://www.comply360.in/labor-law-library/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/THE-MAHARASHTRA-MINIMUM-WAGES-RULES-1963-.pdf); [Gujarat MW Rules 1961 PDF](https://www.comply360.in/labor-law-library/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/GUJARAT-MINIMUM-WAGES-RULES-1961-.pdf)
- **CLRA (Central) Rules 1971, rule 80:** "all the registers and other records shall be preserved in original for a period of **three calendar years** from the date of last entry therein" — [CLRA Central Rules 1971 PDF](https://www.comply360.in/labor-law-library/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/The-Contract-Labour-Regulation-and-Abolition-Central-Rules-1971.pdf)
- **Code on Wages (Central) Rules 2026, rule 51:** "the registers maintained under these rules shall be preserved for a period of **five years** after the date of last entry" — [Lawspedia rule 51](https://www.lawspedia.in/rule-51-of-the-code-on-wages-central-rules-2026)
- **OSH (Central) Rules 2026, rule 72:** all registers and records kept in original for **five calendar years** from the date of last entry; a further sub-rule provides that registers may **not be destroyed after that period unless properly transferred to a new register** — [Lawspedia rule 72](https://www.lawspedia.in/rule-72-of-the-occupational-safety-health-and-working-conditions-central-rules-2026); [LexComply registers and returns matrix](https://lexcomply.com/feeds/employer-registers-returns-matrix) (same five-year test quoted for the accident register)
- **Ministry of Labour Compliance Handbook (Feb 2026):** under the new Codes "the record shall be preserved for period of 5 years" — [labour.gov.in handbook PDF](https://www.labour.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/83978455025732b99b0165def80ab171.pdf)
- A commercial guide states registers "must generally be preserved for at least five years from the last entry" under the Code on Wages rules — consistent but secondary — [CFO Matrix minimum wages registers guide](https://cfomatrix.in/insight/payroll-labour-law/minimum-wages-statutory-registers-guide); [Zenia HR contract labour guide 2026](https://www.zeniahr.com/blog/contract-labour-compliance-guide/)
- **Sub-rule numbering is inconsistent across secondary sources** for rule 72 (one matrix puts the wage slip at 72(2), gazette-based texts put preservation at 72(2) and the Code-on-Wages deeming at 72(3)) — [LexComply matrix](https://lexcomply.com/feeds/employer-registers-returns-matrix) vs [Lawspedia rule 72](https://www.lawspedia.in/rule-72-of-the-occupational-safety-health-and-working-conditions-central-rules-2026)

### Inferences
- **Build retention as five years from last entry, with a per-register override.** Five years is the ceiling among verified figures and satisfies the three-year legacy requirement. The OSH "may not be destroyed unless properly transferred to a new register" rule argues for **never hard-deleting** register data — archive and supersede instead.
- "From the date of last entry" (not from period end) means retention clocks must be driven by the **latest mutation timestamp on the register**, not the register's month. Worth getting right in the data model.

### Gaps
- **The "eight years" figure in the brief could not be verified from any source.** Payment of Bonus retention in particular was not found; Form C has no retention period in any source retrieved. (Eight years is a familiar figure in Indian practice — it is the Companies Act 2013 s.128(5) books-of-account period — so this may be a cross-contamination of regimes. Needs checking.)
- **Per-state retention for Telangana, Karnataka, Kerala and Haryana S&E rules was not retrieved at all.** These may differ from three/five years.
- Payment of Wages Act, Maternity Benefit Act, Equal Remuneration Act and ISMW retention periods were not individually retrieved.

## Q6. Inspection workflow and penalty exposure

### Takeaway
The web-based randomised inspection model is real and operating through Shram Suvidha (the EPFO SOP
confirms the mechanics: central assignment, **date not disclosed to the establishment**), and under the
Codes the officer is an **Inspector-cum-Facilitator** with a statutory duty on the employer to
**produce the registers on demand**. However, **no official document was found that prescribes an
order, bundle or checklist of registers to be produced** — this appears not to exist in published form.
Penalty figures are contested: ₹50,000 is the Code on Wages s.54 figure for underpayment; sources
differ on whether record-keeping contraventions attract ₹20,000 or ₹50,000.

### Cited Findings
- **EPFO Standard Operating Procedure for Inspection of Establishments (31 July 2023):** "On the basis of this list of establishments and deputed official as per system, Regional Head will assign inspection for the establishment through Shram Suvidha Portal **without sharing the date of inspection with establishment**" — [EPFO SOP PDF](https://www.epfindia.gov.in/site_docs/PDFs/Circulars/Y2023-2024/SOP_inspection_CAIU_31072023.pdf)
- The portal uses **random computerised allocation of inspectors**; inspections are described as "randomized, transparent" — [Shram Suvidha, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shram_Suvidha); [Kanakkupillai, Shram Suvidha portal](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/shram-suvidha-portal/); [Shramev Jayate Yojana, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shramew_Jayate_Yojana)
- Under the Codes the **Inspector-cum-Facilitator** has both advisory and enforcement powers and may conduct **web-based, randomly allotted inspections** — [GHR Consultancy Shram Suvidha registration guide 2026](https://www.ghrconsultancy.com/blog/shram-suvidha-registration-guide); [HRMantra Shram Suvidha guide](https://www.hrmantra.com/guide/9%20essential%20steps%20to%20master%20shram%20suvidha%20portal%20registration.html)
- **OSH (Central) Rules rule 72** obliges the employer to **produce the registers on demand before the Inspector-cum-Facilitator** — [Lawspedia rule 72](https://www.lawspedia.in/rule-72-of-the-occupational-safety-health-and-working-conditions-central-rules-2026)
- The inspector prepares the report **at the work spot**, the report is shared with the employer, and the employer has **15 days to comply or respond** — **single secondary source, unverified** — [InCorpX guide to EPFO/ESIC inspection notices](https://www.incorpx.io/guide/how-to-respond-epfo-esic-inspection-notice)
- Reports are **uploaded within 72 hours** by labour inspectors — low-quality general source, treat as unverified — [Unacademy static GK, Shram Suvidha](https://unacademy.com/content/railway-exam/study-material/static-gk/shram-suvidha-portal/)
- **Penalties — Code on Wages:** s.54 fine for paying less than due **"may extend to fifty thousand rupees"**; a **repeat wage offence within five years** carries up to **three months' imprisonment, fine up to ₹1 lakh, or both**; other contraventions a fine up to **₹20,000** — [Corrida Legal, Code on Wages penalties](https://corridalegal.com/code-on-wages-penalties-non-compliance-consequences-for-employers/)
- **Conflict:** one source says the final central rules set a first-time fine of up to **₹20,000**, "a change from earlier drafts which mentioned ₹50,000"; a payroll blog attributes a ₹50,000 fine to non-maintenance of records under ss.50/54, which looks like a misreading since s.54 addresses underpayment — [key4comply overtime laws 2026](https://www.key4comply.com/blogposts/overtime-laws-in-india-2026-the-new-labour-codes-penalties-and-your-right-to-double-pay/) vs [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/payslip-labour-code-2025-mandatory-components-digital-issuance)
- One commentary notes the Codes collapse dozens of overlapping registers into a handful of combined ones maintainable electronically or physically, **but is based on draft rules** — [CalcGuru, labour code registers and returns](https://calcguru.in/labour-code-registers-returns/)
- **Procedural note:** the CLRA inspection procedure as published by a state (Jharkhand) gives a flavour of the pre-Code approach — [Jharkhand Shramadhan, CLRA 1970 inspection procedure PDF](https://shramadhan.jharkhand.gov.in/ftp/WebAdmin/documents/TheContractLabour(Regulation%20and%20Abolition)Act1970.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Product conclusion: build an "inspection bundle" feature anyway.** Since no prescribed order exists, the defensible design is a dated, indexed PDF pack — cover page with establishment identity and registration numbers, then registers in a stable order (employee → attendance/muster → wages/overtime/deductions → leave → statutory abstracts → licences/registrations), with a contents page. That is what an inspector will accept and what a firm can defend; it is a product convention, not a legal requirement, and should be labelled as such in the UI.
- Because the inspection date is deliberately **not disclosed**, the product value is "always inspection-ready" — i.e. registers closed and signed monthly, not generated on demand at inspection time. That argues for a monthly close-and-attest workflow.
- The penalty exposure that matters commercially is **repeat-offence escalation (imprisonment + ₹1 lakh)** rather than the first-instance fine, which strengthens the case for an audit trail showing when each register was closed and by whom.

### Gaps
- **No official Ministry of Labour document was found listing the registers to be produced at a randomised inspection, nor any prescribed order or bundle.** Likely does not exist in published form; the next step would be the Code's inspection-scheme notification under the SS Code / OSH Code.
- **No state-portal inspection requirements were retrieved** for Telangana, Karnataka (karmikaspandana), Kerala or Haryana.
- The 15-day response window and the 72-hour upload timeline are both single-sourced and unverified.
- **Penalty exposure under the old Acts** (Minimum Wages s.22A, Payment of Wages s.20, CLRA s.23/24, state S&E penal sections) was not retrieved at all.
- The 2026 central rules' own penalty figure for register non-maintenance is unresolved (₹20,000 vs ₹50,000).

## Q7. Authentication details the generator must get right

### Takeaway
**This question is largely unanswered from the sources available.** No source retrieved specified who
must sign each register, whether a seal is required, whether every page must be initialled, or where
the registration number and address must appear. The only firm anchors are that registers must be
**produced on demand before the Inspector-cum-Facilitator**, that an **electronic record must be
printable on demand**, and that the old Acts distinguish "occupier"/"manager" (Factories) from
"employer" (S&E/Wages) as the responsible person.

### Cited Findings
- OSH (Central) Rules rule 72 places the production duty on **the employer** — [Lawspedia rule 72](https://www.lawspedia.in/rule-72-of-the-occupational-safety-health-and-working-conditions-central-rules-2026)
- Code on Wages (Central) Rules rule 51 places the maintenance duty on **"the employer of all establishments to which the Code applies"** — [Lawspedia rule 51](https://www.lawspedia.in/rule-51-of-the-code-on-wages-central-rules-2026)
- Telangana S&E Rules rule 29 phrases each obligation as **"Every employer shall maintain..."** — [greytHR Form XXII](https://www.greythr.com/wiki/compliances/telangana-shops-and-establishments-act-form-xxii-register-of-employment/)
- Karnataka S&CE Rules: **"The employer shall maintain a Leave with Wages Register in Form F"** — [Karnataka S&CE Rules 1963 PDF](https://www.comply360.in/labor-law-library/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/THE-KARNATAKA-SHOPS-AND-COMMERCIAL-ESTABLISHMENTS-RULES-1963-.pdf)
- Karnataka registration: application in **Form A within 30 days** of commencing business; **registration certificate in Form C** — [Karnataka S&CE Rules 1963 PDF](https://www.comply360.in/labor-law-library/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/THE-KARNATAKA-SHOPS-AND-COMMERCIAL-ESTABLISHMENTS-RULES-1963-.pdf); [BCL India, Karnataka S&CE Act](https://bclindia.in/karnataka-shops-commercial-establishments-act-know/)
- Printout must be available to the Inspector on demand where records are electronic — [Labour Laws (Amendment) Act 2014 PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2014/the-labour-laws-(amendment)-act,-2014.pdf)

### Inferences
- The responsible signatory is the **"employer"** as defined in each Act — which in a company usually means a person **authorised in writing by the board/management**, named in the S&E registration. For the product, capture an **authorised signatory** per client establishment (name, designation, authority reference) and print it in the signature block; "occupier"/"manager" terminology is a **Factories Act** concept and should not be applied to an IT/services S&E establishment.
- Even without a rule mandating it, **printing the establishment's registration number, registered name and address on every register page is strictly defensible and costless**, and most published state forms carry name/address fields in the header. Do it by default.
- **Seals and per-page initialling** are near-universal *practice* in Indian labour inspections and are harmless to support. Recommend building them as optional print elements (seal placeholder, per-page initial box) rather than asserting they are required.

### Gaps
- **Whose signature goes on each register, per Act and per state: not established from any source.** This is a material product gap and must be resolved by reading the actual form footers in the state rules — the prescribed forms themselves usually carry the signature wording ("Signature of the employer / manager with date").
- **Seal requirement: not established.**
- **Per-page initialling requirement: not established.**
- **Whether the registration number and address must appear in a prescribed place: not established.**
- Recommend the product team obtain the **actual prescribed form images/PDFs** from labour.telangana.gov.in, labour.karnataka.gov.in, lc.kerala.gov.in and hrylabour.gov.in — the form footers answer all of Q7 definitively and nothing short of them will.

## Q8. Content rules a generator can get wrong

### Takeaway
Overtime at **not less than twice the normal rate** is **section 14 of the Code on Wages 2019**
(previously s.14 of the Minimum Wages Act 1948), and — importantly — it is **limited to employees whose
minimum rate of wages has been fixed under the Code**; if no minimum rate is fixed, s.14 does not reach
them. The wage register under rule 51 is **Form IV** and expressly carries overtime, advances, fines and
deductions, with instalment recovery capped at **50% of the wages of the wage period**. Wage slips must
be issued **on or before the day of payment**. The fate of **Equal Remuneration Form D is unresolved**.

### Cited Findings
- **Overtime — Code on Wages s.14:** employer "must pay for every hour or part of an hour worked in excess, at an **overtime rate not less than twice the normal rate of wages**"; the section covers "an employee whose minimum rate of wages has been fixed under that Code", and **if no minimum rate has been fixed, section 14 does not reach them** — [Attendo, overtime rules under the labour codes](https://attendohr.com/blog/overtime-rules-work-under-labour-codes); [Wisemonk, overtime laws in India](https://www.wisemonk.io/blogs/overtime-laws-in-india); [key4comply overtime laws 2026](https://www.key4comply.com/blogposts/overtime-laws-in-india-2026-the-new-labour-codes-penalties-and-your-right-to-double-pay/)
- **Form IV** is the Register of Wages, Overtime, Advances, Fines and Deductions for Damage and Loss; fines and deductions must be logged there — [Lawspedia rule 51](https://www.lawspedia.in/rule-51-of-the-code-on-wages-central-rules-2026)
- **Recovery cap:** instalments in a wage period "shall not exceed **fifty per cent** of the wages of the employee", with particulars recorded in **Form IV** — [Lawspedia rule 51](https://www.lawspedia.in/rule-51-of-the-code-on-wages-central-rules-2026); [Taxmann Wages (Central) Rules 2026 guide](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/wages-central-rules-2026-code-on-wages-chapter-wise-guide)
- **Wage period:** under the Wages rules, the wage period "on a daily basis is defined as 8 hours"; the longer wage period for minimum-wage purposes is **monthly (rule 9)** — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/wages-central-rules-2026-code-on-wages-chapter-wise-guide); [Acuity Law FAQs on Code on Wages and the 2026 Central Rules](https://acuitylaw.co.in/faqs/faqs-on-code-on-wages-2019-and-the-code-on-wages-central-rules-2026/)
- **Wage slip timing:** s.50(3) requires wage slips in prescribed form issued to all employees **on or before payment of wages**; an older parallel is MW (Central) Rules Form XI, issued **"at least a day prior to the disbursement of wages"** — [Corrida Legal](https://corridalegal.com/code-on-wages-penalties-non-compliance-consequences-for-employers/); [Minimum Wages (Central) Rules 1950, indiankanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/130387733/)
- **Muster roll timing (old rule, likely carried forward in substance):** MW (Central) Rules **rule 26(5)** — muster roll kept **at the work-spot** and attendance recorded **daily within three hours of the shift starting** — [indiankanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/130387733/)
- **Form IX** (attendance register-cum-muster roll) is described as "the evidentiary backbone for overtime, leave and wage-period disputes" — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/wages-central-rules-2026-code-on-wages-chapter-wise-guide)
- **Equal Remuneration Act 1976 was repealed on 21 November 2025** via s.69(1) of the Code on Wages; the gender-pay rule now sits in **s.3** of the Code (no discrimination on ground of gender in wages for same or similar work, and in recruitment) — [Taxmann, ERA repealed by Code on Wages](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/equal-remuneration-act-1976-repealed-by-code-on-wages/); [ZeniaHR, ERA repealed: what replaced it](https://www.zeniahr.com/repealed/equal-remuneration-act-1976/); [Code on Wages 2019, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_on_Wages,_2019)
- **Form D (Equal Remuneration) — unresolved.** Form D was required under **s.8 of the ERA 1976 read with rule 6 of the Equal Remuneration Rules 1976** (remuneration component-wise and consolidated, workers' information gender-wise). **No Code on Wages provision or rule was found that expressly replaces or preserves Form D**; one employer-compliance page still presents Form D as current — [greytHR Form D](https://www.greythr.com/wiki/compliances/central-form-d-register-be-maintained-employer/); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/equal-remuneration-act-1976-repealed-by-code-on-wages/)
- **Karnataka Form T** is the combined muster roll and register of wages and "contains attendance particulars including over time particulars" — though one blog describes it only as a daily attendance register, which is narrower than the other sources — [keka Karnataka guide](https://www.keka.com/compliance/acts/karnataka-shops-and-establishments-act); [greytHR Karnataka](https://www.greythr.com/wiki/acts/karnataka-shops-commercial-establishments-act/)
- The Code on Wages rules also contemplate a **separate wage slip, reported as Form V**, alongside the three registers — secondary, and in tension with other sources calling the wage slip Form XVI — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/wages-central-rules-2026-code-on-wages-chapter-wise-guide); [LexComply matrix](https://lexcomply.com/feeds/employer-registers-returns-matrix)
- Reference templates circulating for the 2026 regime — orientation only, not authority — [Attendo statutory register formats template](https://attendohr.com/templates-checklist/statutory-register-formats-template)

### Inferences
- **The s.14 scoping point is the single biggest correctness trap for an IT/services product.** Most of a 400-person IT firm's staff are above any scheduled minimum wage, and several Codes/old Acts scope overtime and some register columns to employees with a *fixed minimum rate*. A generator that applies 2x overtime universally will be wrong; one that omits it for covered staff will be wrong too. The product needs a per-employee "minimum-wage-covered" flag driven by the state's scheduled employment and wage ceiling, and should surface the determination to the preparer rather than silently deciding.
- **Form D should be generated but flagged.** Given the ERA is repealed and no successor register was found, the defensible product behaviour is: stop asserting Form D is required post-21 Nov 2025, keep the generator available for pre-repeal periods and for states still on deemed-old-rules, and ensure **Form IV already captures gender-wise remuneration** so the s.3 non-discrimination evidence exists regardless.
- Mid-month joiners/leavers and LOP coding are **not addressed by any source found** — but the structure of Form IX (attendance-cum-muster roll, daily entries) and Form IV (wages for the wage period) implies the correct treatment: **every person employed at any time during the wage period must appear**, with date of joining/leaving in the employee register (Form I) and daily non-attendance marks in Form IX driving the wage computation in Form IV. Showing only month-end headcount would break the muster-roll logic. This is an inference, not a cited rule.

### Gaps
- **How a mid-month joiner or leaver is to be shown: no source found.** Needs the actual Form I/IV/IX layouts from the gazette.
- **Whether a register must list every employee in the period or only those on the rolls at month end: no source found** (inference above only).
- **How LOP/absence codes are to be recorded: no source found.** The prescribed forms' legends (usually printed on the form itself — P/A/H/W/CL/EL/LOP) are the answer and were not retrieved.
- **Payment-date rules** (Code on Wages s.17 wage-payment timelines — 7th/10th day, and on termination) were not retrieved in this session, though they are well-established in the Code.
- Whether the wage slip is Form V or Form XVI is unresolved.

## Q9. Official e-register templates or portals publishing machine-readable form layouts

### Takeaway
**No official machine-readable form layouts were found.** The Ministry publishes a **Compliance Handbook
for Employers Under the Four Labour Codes (Feb 2026)** and the gazetted rules carry the forms as
appendices in PDF; Shram Suvidha handles **registration, returns and inspection assignment**, not
register templates. State labour departments publish form PDFs/HTML, not schemas.

### Cited Findings
- **Ministry of Labour & Employment, "Compliance Handbook for Employers Under the Four Labour Codes"** — [labour.gov.in handbook PDF (uploaded Feb 2026)](https://www.labour.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/83978455025732b99b0165def80ab171.pdf)
- **Forms are appendices to the rules:** rule 51 refers to "the formats appended to these rules" — i.e. PDF annexures in the gazette, not a published schema — [Lawspedia rule 51](https://www.lawspedia.in/rule-51-of-the-code-on-wages-central-rules-2026)
- **Shram Suvidha** is the unified compliance platform for **registration (EPF/ESIC/CLRA), return filing and inspection allocation** — [Shram Suvidha, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shram_Suvidha); [GHR Consultancy registration guide 2026](https://www.ghrconsultancy.com/blog/shram-suvidha-registration-guide)
- **Returns are electronic**: Code on Wages rule 48 returns filed electronically in forms under the OSH Code; OSH annual return **Form XVII** by the last day of February — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/wages-central-rules-2026-code-on-wages-chapter-wise-guide); [LexComply matrix](https://lexcomply.com/feeds/employer-registers-returns-matrix)
- **State form sources (HTML/PDF only):** Telangana rules and forms at [labour.telangana.gov.in S&E Rules 1990](https://labour.telangana.gov.in/content/links/THE%20TELANGANA%20SHOPS%20&%20ESTABLISHMENT%20RULES%201990.htm) and the [Telangana checklist/downloads page](https://labour.telangana.gov.in/Checklist.do); Kerala Act text at [Kerala S&CE Act 1960 PDF](https://banyanhr.com/DOWNLOADS/LABOUR%20LAW/SHOPS%20AND%20COMMERCIAL%20ESTABLISHMENTS%20ACT%201960%20KERALA.pdf); Karnataka rules at [Karnataka S&CE Rules 1963 PDF](https://www.comply360.in/labor-law-library/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/THE-KARNATAKA-SHOPS-AND-COMMERCIAL-ESTABLISHMENTS-RULES-1963-.pdf)
- **Gazette tracking**: the Code on Wages (Central) Rules 2026 are indexed as `CG-DL-E-08052026-272365` — [Gazette Tracker](https://gazettetracker.com/g/CG-DL-E-08052026-272365)
- **Compliance-vendor form libraries** exist and are useful for orientation only, never authority — [comply4hr MW Form X .doc](https://comply4hr.com/docs/cen/CEMWA/CEMWAFX.doc); [greytHR compliance wiki](https://www.greythr.com/wiki/compliances/central-register-wages-form-xvii/); [ComplianceAge library, Code on Wages (Central) Rules 2026](https://updates.complianceage.com/library-document/code-on-wages-central-rules-2026/); [ServeHR Code on Wages Rules PDF](https://servehrindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/COW-Rules-Serve-HR.pdf)

### Inferences
- The product will have to **derive its own form schemas from gazette PDFs** and maintain them as versioned templates with an effective-from/effective-to date per state and per Act. There is no upstream machine-readable source to sync from. A "form version registry" (Act, state, form number, effective dates, gazette reference, column list) is the right internal abstraction.
- Because the Codes' forms are new (8 May 2026) and four target states are still on draft rules, the registry must support **two concurrent live versions per state** and must record the gazette citation for each, so generated output can print its own authority line.

### Gaps
- No official XML/JSON/XSD/API for any labour register form layout was found.
- The Ministry's compliance handbook could not be opened; it may contain the consolidated register/return matrix that would be the best single input to the product's form registry. **Opening this PDF should be the first follow-up action.**

## Cross-cutting notes for product design

### Cited Findings
- Vendor matrices attempting the "which registers in 2026" question exist and show how contested the area is — [LexComply, which registers and returns must an Indian employer maintain in 2026](https://lexcomply.com/feeds/employer-registers-returns-matrix); [CalcGuru, what replaces your old state formats](https://calcguru.in/labour-code-registers-returns/); [TalentCompliance, labour law registers every employer must maintain](https://talentcompliance.in/resources/blog/labour-law-registers-every-employer-must-maintain-in-india); [LawChatter, maintenance of registers under different labour laws](https://lawchatter.in/info/company-law/judiciary/maintenance-of-registers-under-different-labour-laws)
- Paybooks and Remunance summarise the employer-impact and state-rules dependency — [Paybooks labour code implementation](https://paybooks.in/article/labour-code-implementation-in-india/); [Remunance, India labour codes 2026](https://remunance.com/blog/india-labour-codes-2026/); [Lawrbit, India's new labour codes](https://www.lawrbit.com/article/indias-new-labour-codes/)

### Inferences
- **Do not ship absolute compliance claims.** Every generated register should print its own authority line (Act, rule, form number, gazette reference, effective date) and the product should expose a "verified against gazette on {date}" field per template. Given how much of the above is vendor-sourced, that is the only honest posture.
- **The highest-value engineering decision** is a canonical employee/attendance/wage/deduction/leave dataset plus a dated, per-state form-template registry. Everything else (Forms A–E, I/IV/IX, XIII/XIV/XV, XXII/XXIII/XXV, T/F/H, A/B/BB/F, D/E) is a rendering concern.
- **Counsel sign-off is required** before go-live on: per-state form numbering for Kerala and Haryana; whether any of the four states requires bound/paper or local-language registers; who signs each register; and whether the client's employees are within s.14 minimum-wage coverage.

### Gaps
- The brief's four specific premises that could **not** be corroborated and may be wrong: **Kerala Form XIV**, **Haryana Form XIII**, **eight-year retention**, and **"Rule 26-27"** as the handle for the combined-register regime (the relevant provisions found were MW (Central) Rules rules 26(5)/26A and CLRA rule 80, not a "rule 26-27" pair).
- Because no page could be fetched, **nothing in this document is verified at primary source.** Treat the whole file as a research scaffold with citations to follow up, not as a settled statement of law.
